## Bottom line

The evidence supports manufacturing as a small satellite next to a diversified core, bought in instalments, not as a core holding. On a weighted score, a low-cost index fund comes first; among active funds, Kotak has the strongest case and ICICI the second. Of 12 active funds, only Kotak and ICICI have beaten the index convincingly over more than three years. The other ten are too young, depend on one lucky stretch, are too costly, or are run by a new manager.

**The five facts that drive this:**

1. **The theme has beaten the market by a wide margin, but it falls harder.** The index fund returned 16.6% a year over 3 years vs 9.9% for the Nifty 500. It fell 23.2% in 2024–25 vs 17.6% for the Nifty 500.
2. **Late buyers earned little.** A 2-year SIP in the index fund made 2.5% a year. Nine of the 12 active funds launched near the 2024 peak.
3. **The price is fair, so returns now depend on earnings.** The index P/E is 25.6 against a 5-year median of 26.9. Earnings must grow in the mid-teens through a crude, rupee and rate shock: Brent above $117, rupee near 96, repo raised to 5.50%.
4. **The rules are about to reshape these funds.** SEBI's 50% overlap cap, with a three-year glide path or forced mergers, will push the near-index funds (HDFC, Axis, ICICI, Baroda) toward smaller, less liquid stocks.
5. **Manager risk is everywhere.** Nine of the 12 active funds changed or added a manager in the last year. ABSL's strong 3-year record was built by someone who no longer runs it.

**Scorecard summary:** index fund 4.10, Kotak 4.00, ICICI 3.60, Axis 3.50, HDFC 3.20 … ABSL 2.10, Motilal 2.05, Quant 1.60.

One caveat for anyone who already holds flexi- or multi-cap funds: Reliance, M&M, Maruti and Sun Pharma are probably already in them, so the case for adding this theme is weaker.

## First principles: what you are actually buying

{{< chart src="m01-at-a-glance.svg" label="At a glance" source="My calculations from daily NAVs on mfapi.in (AMFI data), direct plans, to 30 Oct 2026; IndexPE; Groww" >}}

A manufacturing fund is the Indian market with banks, IT, telecom, retail and most services taken out. What remains is about 70% cyclical: autos, capital goods, metals and energy. Its return comes from three things: earnings growth, the multiple the market pays, and the rupee and commodity prices that move both.

**How the benchmark is built** ([Nippon ETF factsheet](https://mf.nipponindiaim.com/FundsAndPerformance/ProductNotes/NipponIndia-Nifty-India-Manufacturing-ETF-Aug-2026.pdf)):

- Universe: Nifty 100, Midcap 150 and Smallcap 50, filtered to manufacturing industries.
- Selection: large caps first, then mid and small caps are added until each industry reaches 75% coverage of its free-float value.
- Weights: free-float market cap, capped at 5% per stock. Autos and capital goods each have a floor of 20%.
- Rebalanced every March and September. As of September 2026 it held 77 stocks ([IndexPE](https://www.indexpe.in/nifty-india-manufacturing)).

**What it holds (30 Sep 2026):** autos 25.3%, capital goods 23.1%, healthcare 18.5%, metals and mining 14.7%, oil and gas 7.6%, chemicals 6.2%. Two small sectors make up the remaining 4.6%. The top five stocks are M&M 5.0%, Reliance 5.0%, Sun Pharma 4.6%, Maruti 3.7% and Tata Steel 3.7%.

{{< chart src="n02-index-build.svg" label="Figure 1" source="Nippon India ETF factsheet (Aug 2026); IndexPE · weights at 30 Sep 2026" >}}

**Three consequences that follow from this build:**

1. **The 20% floors fix a cyclical tilt.** Even a passive manufacturing fund must hold at least 40% in autos and capital goods. Both are rate- and capex-sensitive, so it cannot be a defensive holding.
2. **It overlaps heavily with what you already own.** Reliance, M&M, Maruti, Sun Pharma, Tata Steel and L&T are core holdings of most Nifty 50 and flexi-cap funds. A manufacturing fund raises your existing tilt more than it diversifies.
3. **Return = earnings + re-rating, and the two can cancel.** In the year to 30 Oct 2026, earnings of the Nifty500 Multicap Manufacturing index grew 8.5%. Its P/E fell from 30.7 to 27.6, so the price ended 2.5% lower ([IndexPE](https://www.indexpe.in/nifty500-multicap-india-manufacturing-50-30-20)). Businesses improved while shareholders lost money because the starting multiple was too high.

{{< chart src="n03-earnings-vs-rerating.svg" label="Figure 2" source="IndexPE, Nifty500 Multicap India Manufacturing 50:30:20 · year to 30 Oct 2026" >}}

Second-order point: a fund is only as good as the gap between earnings growth and what the price already assumes. That gap matters more than the government's manufacturing push.

## Performance against the true benchmark

Only ICICI and Kotak have beaten the index fund convincingly over more than three years, by about 3.7–3.8 points a year, and both also fell less in down months. The rest are too young to judge, or their lead came in one burst rather than steadily. All figures below are my own calculations from daily NAVs ([mfapi.in](https://www.mfapi.in/), AMFI data) to 30 Oct 2026, using the Mirae Nifty India Manufacturing ETF as the benchmark. Third-party sites that show a "benchmark" of −7.3% for 1 year are actually showing the Nifty 500.

**The theme itself has beaten the market, with a catch.** Over 3 years the manufacturing index fund returned 16.6% a year, against 9.9% for a Nifty 500 index fund and 7.4% for a Nifty 50 fund. Since Feb 2022 it beat the Nifty 500 by 6.1 points a year and in 87% of rolling 1-year windows. But in sharp falls it fell harder: −23.2% vs −17.6% in 2024–25, and −12.9% vs −9.6% in Aug–Oct 2026.

**What the numbers say beneath the headline:**

- **A burst is not a skill.** Invesco's +6.2 points mostly came in the last six months (+10.7% vs −3.8% for the index), and it beat the index in only 30% of 1-year windows. ABSL beat it in only 35%; its 3-year lead was made in 2025–26 by small- and mid-cap picks, under a manager who left in May 2026.
- **The theme can lag for two years in a row.** ABSL lost 12.1% in 2018 and 3.2% in 2019, while a Nifty 50 fund made 4.3% and 13.3%.
- **Investor experience was poor for late buyers.** A 2-year SIP in the index fund earned 2.5% a year; in a Nifty 500 fund, −2.0%. Only the funds with a mid- and small-cap tilt (ABSL, Axis, Invesco, Kotak) made double digits.
- **ICICI's long record is real but cyclical.** It returned 20.4% a year over 7 years vs 13.1% for the Nifty 500, yet fell 37.6% in the 2020 crash, no better than the market.

**What this means:**

- **The near-index funds are expensive index funds.** HDFC, Axis and Baroda move 0.97 with the index. Their TERs of 0.92–1.31% buy 4–5 points of tracking error. HDFC's 2.4 years of history show a 1.8-point lead but only a 39% hit rate on 1-year windows.

- **ICICI uses its leeway on construction and cement.** It holds L&T 3.3% and UltraTech 2.9%. This is a sensible tilt to the capex cycle, but it is not pure manufacturing.
- **HDFC is the cheap-P/E fund for a reason.** Oil marketing companies (HPCL, BPCL) and metals keep its P/E low, and both are hit by Brent above $110 (next section).

## Fund-house constraints: the rules that shape what you own

The rule that matters most is new. SEBI's 26 Feb 2026 circular caps a thematic fund's overlap with the same fund house's other equity schemes at 50%, with large-cap funds exempt. Funds that cannot get under the cap within three years will be merged away. Over the next two years, the big near-index funds will have to become something different from what investors bought.

| Rule | What it says | What it means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |

**Second-order effects of these rules:**

1. **The theme will get more small-cap and less liquid.** Overlap rules push funds away from the shared heavyweights, and group limits stop big fund houses from scaling in small caps. The likely result is more mid- and small-cap names, held in many funds, in a category with no liquidity stress-test disclosure like the one small-cap funds publish.
2. **Already-different funds need the fewest changes.** Invesco, LIC, Quant and Motilal have the lowest correlation with the index, so the cap is likely to bind them least. The near-index funds of large fund houses (HDFC, Axis, ICICI, Baroda) are likely to need the most. This is my inference; check each fund house's monthly overlap disclosure.
3. **Track records reset.** A fund that changes manager and is forced to restructure its portfolio is effectively new. That applies most to ABSL and ICICI, and to HDFC's portfolio.

## Today's market: strong factories, a cost and funding shock

Indian factories are busy, but their costs and their cost of money have jumped at the same time. Volumes are fine: PMI 55.1, manufacturing IIP +7.6%, and capacity use above the 75% level that usually triggers new capex. Margins are the risk: Brent is above $117, the rupee is near 95–97 per dollar, and the RBI hiked rates on 7 Oct. For the next two quarters, earnings revisions matter more than volumes.

**Sector by sector: first- and second-order effects** (weights from the index; company examples from Q2 FY27 results)

- **Healthcare, 19%.** India sales are growing 12–20%, but US generics are weak (Cipla and Dr Reddy's profit down about 35%). Industry profit is down 10%. Second order: a weak rupee helps only if US pricing holds. The large positions in Divi's and CDMO names (Sai Life, Rubicon) are bets on contract manufacturing, not generics.
- **Metals, 15%.** Steel profits are up 27–117% on higher prices and a weak rupee. Second order: the US charges 50% on steel and aluminium, and a global slowdown would reverse prices fast. This is the most cyclical 15% of the index.
- **Oil and gas, 8%.** Oil marketers' Q2 profit rose 164%, helped by inventory gains as crude rose. Second order: if crude stays above $110 and pump prices stay frozen, Q3 turns into losses on every litre sold. HDFC (HPCL and BPCL 3.5%) and ICICI (Reliance 5.9%) carry this.
- **Chemicals, 6%.** Specialty chemicals profit +20%, but Chinese overcapacity is hitting names like SRF. Feedstock is crude-linked.

## Future India: what is structural and what is already priced

The direction is real, but the national target is a weak guide for investors. Manufacturing is about 17% of GDP. It peaked at 18.5% in FY22 and was 17.3% in FY24 ([BizzBuzz](https://www.bizzbuzz.news/manufacturing/india-makes-more-but-not-fast-enough-1389898)). The National Manufacturing Mission targets 25% by 2035 ([PIB budget note](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/feb/doc2026212786601.pdf)). Reaching that means manufacturing must outgrow the whole economy by about 4% a year for ten years, which it has not done over the past decade (the share stayed between 15% and 18.5%). Make in India set the same 25% goal in 2014. The money will be made in a few segments, not in "manufacturing" as a whole.

Several PLI windows end in FY27, so margins will need to stand without the incentive.

**What the price already assumes.** At a P/E of 25.6, near its 5-year median, the index is not paying for a re-rating. Returns from here will roughly track earnings growth plus a small dividend. Last year that growth was 8.5% for the multicap manufacturing index. A mid-teens return needs mid-teens EPS growth, which needs margins to hold through this cost shock. Second order: the long-run story is right, but the next 12 months will be decided by margins and crude, not by policy.

## How I would use this theme

This is how I read the evidence, not a recommendation. I would treat manufacturing as a satellite of at most 10–15% of equity, bought in instalments, through an index fund or Kotak. The theme is fairly valued but faces a margin shock, it is already part of any diversified fund, and only one active fund has shown skill, low cost and downside protection together.

**How I would enter.** Park the amount in a liquid fund and move it in over 6–12 monthly instalments.

- **Speed up** if Brent falls below $95, or the index P/E drops below about 23 (roughly 15% under its 5-year median), or Q3 results (Jan–Feb 2027) show margins holding.
- **Pause** if Brent stays above $125 or the rupee crosses 100 per dollar. Both would push the bear case.

**The review rules I would use.**

- **Fund level:** review on any lead-manager exit; if the fund's 1-year hit rate against the index drops below 40% over three years; if correlation with the index jumps above 0.98 (you are paying active fees for an index); or if the fund shows overlap above 50% with no plan to cut it.
- **Theme level:** trim if the index P/E goes above about 32 (20% over its median) or the PMI stays below 50 for two months. Add if capacity use goes back above 77% while the P/E is still near the median.

## Method, gaps and sources

**Method.** Returns, drawdowns, rolling windows, capture ratios, information ratios and SIP returns were calculated from complete daily NAV histories of 23 schemes (direct growth plans) from [mfapi.in](https://www.mfapi.in/), which republishes AMFI NAVs, through 30 Oct 2026. **Gaps and conflicts**

- Portfolios are as of 30 Sep 2026, from sharpely and Groww, not the AMC Excel files. sharpely's holding counts sometimes differ between sections of a page, and its P/E method is not published.

- Full portfolios (for exact pairwise overlap and active share) were not downloaded; correlation and tracking error stand in for active share.
- LIC and Motilal portfolio statistics (cap split, P/E) were not available on the pages read.
- Trendlyne's Q2 industry data covers early reporters only and will change as more results arrive.
- Scenario growth rates, exit P/Es, probabilities and the scorecard weights are my assumptions.
- Not yet checked: each fund's portfolio turnover.

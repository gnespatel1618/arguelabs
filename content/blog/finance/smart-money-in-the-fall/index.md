## The answer in one minute

{{< chart src="s01-at-a-glance.svg" label="At a glance" source="NSDL via PTI, AMFI, NSE India Ownership Report (Jun 2026), exchange block-deal data · as of 26 Oct 2026" >}}

1. **Foreigners are selling and Indian funds are buying.** FPIs sold a record ₹3.08 lakh crore of Indian equities in 2026 so far, including ₹35,861 crore in September and ₹48,056 crore in October to date. Domestic institutions bought ₹64,759 crore in September alone. SIPs hit a record ₹32,490 crore a month.
2. **The biggest trade is a handover in large caps.** FIIs cut stakes and mutual funds took the shares:
   - HDFC Bank: FIIs −2.4 points, domestic institutions +1.7.
   - BSE, MCX and Swiggy saw the same pattern.
   - So did Bharti Airtel, M&M, Muthoot Finance and Indus Towers.
   - In ITC's ₹9,395 crore block deal, GQG sold and Fidelity plus four Indian fund houses bought.
3. **Where both FIIs and Indian institutions added, the list is mostly new-age platforms and special situations:**
   - New listings: Lenskart, Meesho, Groww, Pine Labs.
   - Thyrocare, Karnataka Bank and Apar Industries.
   - Shares that changed hands from a promoter or private-equity seller: EPL, Torrent Pharma, Adani Enterprises.
4. **The one sector FPIs bought in October is IT** (+₹2,599 crore in 1–15 Oct), led by HCL Tech, Tech Mahindra and Infosys stakes. Mutual funds hold IT at a record-low 5.3% of their equity assets. That is the clearest disagreement between the two camps.
5. **Mutual funds' biggest September buys** were:
   - Reliance, about ₹6,000 crore, ahead of the Jio Platforms IPO.
   - BSE, over ₹5,500 crore, on its entry into the Nifty 50.
   - NSE shares before its IPO.
   - Private banks and Swiggy. Their biggest sales were Wipro, L&T and TCS.
6. **Results season backs some of this, not all.** Early Q2 profits are up 25.5% across 337 companies, but metals and power drive much of it. Banks are growing profits while margins shrink, and pharma has disappointed.
7. **Where several signals line up** (institutional buying, a decent quarter and a sane valuation), the short list is HCL Tech, Karnataka Bank, ICICI Bank, Kotak Bank and ITC. HDFC Bank goes on a separate watch list: funds keep absorbing it at its cheapest valuation in years, but its quarter was weak. These are names to research further, not buy calls.

- **September flow, in one line:** FPIs sold ₹45,537 crore in the secondary market but put ₹9,676 crore into IPOs, mostly NSE's. Domestic institutions bought for the 38th month in a row.
- **Savers are buying the dip, not fleeing it.** Equity fund inflows rose 31% in September. The SIP stoppage ratio fell for a fifth straight month, to 78.5%. New money went mostly to small-cap (₹9,039 crore), mid-cap (₹7,835 crore) and flexi-cap (₹6,702 crore) funds. Large-cap funds got just ₹859 crore.
- **Ownership has flipped.** FPIs owned 15.1% of NSE-listed companies in June 2026, the lowest in 69 quarters. Domestic institutions owned 19.5%, and mutual funds alone a record 11.6%. That gap is the widest since 2001.
- **Why FPIs are selling:**
   - US 10-year yields above 5%.
   - The rupee near 95 per dollar.
   - Brent crude at $100–114 because of the West Asia conflict.
   - The RBI's rate hike to 5.50% on 7 October.
   - Money chasing the AI rally in Korea and Taiwan.
   - Valuations that look rich against FY27 earnings growth of around 9–10%.
- **The selling is slowing.** FPIs turned net buyers on 16 October, ending a 15-session streak, and bought on two days in the week to 23 October. Brokers now point to lower US yields, a firmer rupee and FII ownership already at multi-year lows. That is not yet a reversal.

## Where FIIs are selling, and the one place they are buying

{{< chart src="a02-fpi-sectors.svg" label="Figure 5" source="NSDL fortnightly sector-wise FPI reports, 15 Sep, 30 Sep and 15 Oct 2026 · 16–31 Oct not yet published" >}}

- **Financial services was the top sale in every fortnight**, ₹21,781 crore in six weeks. This is the HDFC Bank, BSE, MCX and NBFC selling seen in the shareholding data below.
- **Consumption is being sold hard.** FMCG (−₹8,172 crore) and autos (−₹6,533 crore) were the second- and third-largest sales in early October. That fits the worries about crude oil, the rupee and input costs that hit Nestle's stock after results.
- **IT flipped to a buy in October** (+₹2,599 crore) after TCS, HCL Tech and Wipro reported in mid-October and the Nifty IT index rallied about 12% in a week. It is the only large sector FPIs bought.
- **Healthcare flipped the other way**, from +₹2,114 crore in early September to −₹5,497 crore in early October, as Dr Reddy's and Torrent missed.
- **Consumer services** (Eternal, Swiggy, Lenskart, Meesho, travel) was bought in late September, then sold in October.

**What the cheques say**

- **ITC is the cleanest buy signal of the fall.** One foreign seller (GQG) exited at ₹257, and a long-only foreign fund (Fidelity) plus four Indian fund houses took nearly all of it. The stock is still around ₹258, within 2% of its 52-week low, at about 16× earnings.
- **Indian funds are the capital market now.** They took the largest share of every QIP above, a third of the Jio anchor book and over half of NSE's. Foreign money still shows up through sovereign funds (GIC, ADIA, Norges), which buy on long horizons.
- **Capex and power-equipment names are still raising money from Indian funds** (Bharat Forge, TD Power, Cemindia). Funds are paying up for the investment cycle even in a falling market.
- **The Jio IPO is soaking up liquidity.** ₹30,213 crore had to come from somewhere, and part of the October selling in other large caps is funds raising cash for it. Watch for that money to return after listing on 28 October.

## Results season: are the stocks being bought delivering?

**The aggregate looks strong but is narrow.**

- Profits are up 25.5% year on year across the first 337 companies to report, the best in 12 quarters, and sales are up 22.3%.
- Adani Power, Hindustan Zinc and JSW Steel alone account for about 30% of the profit increase while making only 6% of the sales.
- Brokers' Nifty EPS estimates have barely moved: Motilal Oswal is at ₹1,233 for FY27 and has cut FY28 by 1.3%.
- Beat/miss scorecards come out in early November.

**What this says**

- **IT is where foreign buying and results line up best.** HCL Tech and TCS beat, the sector rallied about 12%, and FPIs turned buyers. Mutual funds are still near a record-low IT weight, so domestic money could still move in.

## The conviction list: where the signals agree

One signal is noise: funds must deploy SIP money, index changes force buying, and promoter sales have to land somewhere. So each stock is checked against four signals:

1. FII stake up in the September quarter.
2. Domestic institutions' stake up.
3. Named fund-manager or block-deal buying in September–October.
4. A Q2 result that held up.

## What smart money is selling, and the traps

**Both camps are selling:**

- **IT laggards:** Wipro (funds cut holdings by two-thirds since December) and, among large caps, TCS and L&T are at the top of September's fund sales.
- **Capital-markets winners after their run:** FIIs cut BSE by 4.2 points and MCX by 2.8 points, though Indian funds took both.
- **Consumption:** FPIs sold ₹11,173 crore of FMCG and ₹12,745 crore of autos in six weeks.
- **Funds' broad cuts:** Adani Ports, Tata Motors PV, Grasim, Jio Financial, RVNL (shares held −42%), Lodha (−25%), IDBI Bank (−45%) and MRPL (−34%).
- **FII exits in mid and small caps:** Genus Power (−11.3 points), HEG (−4.9), LIC Housing Finance (−2.5) and ABFRL (−2.8, with domestic institutions also selling).

**Traps when reading "smart money" data**

1. **The data is stale.** September portfolios and September-quarter shareholding were struck on 30 September, before October's selling, the RBI hike and the smallcap crash in the week to 23 October.
2. **Forced buying is not conviction.** Indian funds must invest roughly ₹38,000 crore a month and track their benchmarks. A +35% jump in fund holdings of BSE is index inclusion, not a view.
3. **Ownership moves are often one-off events:** a promoter or private-equity sale (EPL, Granules, Torrent), a QIP (Adani Enterprises), or new listings settling into index funds (Lenskart, Meesho, Groww, Pine Labs). Check *why* a stake moved before reading anything into it.
4. **Smart money buys expensive stocks too.** Several joint buys trade at 40–170× earnings, and funds bought Dr Reddy's just before a 36% profit fall.
5. **The handover can reverse.** If FPIs return in force, as they did in July–August, the stocks they dumped (HDFC Bank, BSE, consumption) tend to bounce hardest. If they keep selling, Indian funds' ability to absorb rests on SIPs continuing. SIP stoppages are falling for now.
6. **This is a sample, not a census.** The 52 stocks charted above are the ones named in fund, FII and block-deal news, not all 5,000 listed companies.

**How to use it**

- Treat institutional buying as a filter for what to research, not a reason to buy. The edge is in the *agreement* between flows, results and price.

- For SIP investors nothing here changes the playbook: keep investing. This is for people picking individual stocks.

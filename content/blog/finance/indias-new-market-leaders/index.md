Eight companies listed since October 2024 are #1 in their Indian category, worth about ₹23.4 lakh crore together. Leadership is priced very unevenly: at today's prices the market asks Waaree for roughly 8% a year of profit growth and Lenskart for roughly 35%.

The second-order pattern: the leaders that sit on **rails** (NSE, Jio) or own the **customer relationship** (Groww, SBI Funds) keep 50-80% operating margins; the leaders that **make physical goods** (LG, Waaree) keep 10-22%. Lenskart is the exception because it owns all three layers.

## How the eight were picked

A company makes the list only if it passes all three filters below. A company that is #2 in a two-player market (Swiggy, Hyundai India) or a scale player without the top rank (Tata Capital, HDB Financial, Ather) is left out, however large it is.

| Filter | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| New | Listed on NSE/BSE in roughly the last 24 months (late 2024 to Nov 2026) |
| Market leader | #1 in its core Indian category by revenue, volume, users or share, as stated in the DRHP/RHP or an industry report (Redseer, CRISIL, F&S, Technopak) |
| Investable | Market cap above ₹20,000 cr and at least one quarter of results published after listing |

Three ideas run through the whole piece:

- **Leadership is only worth something if it lets the company earn more per rupee than #2.** Each deep dive asks where the profit pool sits in the value chain and whether the leader captures it.
- **Second-order effects matter more than the headline.** Every company section names who loses if the leader keeps winning, and what breaks the moat.
- **Valuation is checked against what the price already assumes.** Rather than only quoting multiples, each section backs out the growth the market price implies.

### 1. Jio Platforms: the toll road on India's data

**Value chain.** Spectrum (government auctions) → network gear (Ericsson, Nokia, Samsung, plus Jio's own 5G stack) → towers and fibre (owned by InvITs that Jio rents from) → **operator: Jio** → apps and devices → 542.4 mn subscribers. Jio sits at the operator layer, which keeps the biggest margin in the chain (54.1% EBITDA) but also carries the spectrum and network bill, so return on equity is only 9.4%.

**Why it leads.** Jio built the lowest cost per GB in the market in 2016 and used it to take share. Today the moat is scale: 300 mn+ 5G users spread the fixed network cost thinner than any rival can.

**Second-order effects.**

- Every ₹10 rise in monthly ARPU is worth about ₹6,500 cr of annual revenue at 542 mn users, and most of it drops to EBITDA because the network cost is already paid.
- Tariff hikes lift Airtel too; the loser is Vodafone Idea, and through it the tower companies that depend on Vi's rentals.
- Home broadband through AirFiber (15.1 mn users) attacks local cable operators, not Airtel.

| Product | For a user: what you get | For an investor: how Jio earns | For a business or partner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| True5G mobile (prepaid, postpaid) | Fast data and calls; 44.2 GB a month is the average use | Monthly recharge; ARPU ₹218.8 | Largest audience for apps and ads |
| JioAirFiber and JioFiber | Home Wi-Fi without digging cables; AirFiber uses 5G to the home | Higher ARPU than mobile; 30 mn+ fixed broadband users | Small offices get broadband without a fibre wait |
| JioBharat phones | A cheap 4G feature phone for first-time users | Moves 2G users onto paid 4G plans | Feeds the bottom of the subscriber funnel |
| JioBusiness and cloud | Connectivity, cloud and devices for firms | Enterprise contracts, early stage | SMBs and large firms buying bundled IT |
| Apps and AI devices (JioSaavn, Jio Call Agent, JioFrames, JioShield) | Music, call summaries, smart glasses, family safety | Add-ons that make the plan stickier and lift ARPU | Developers reach Jio's base |

**Q2 FY27.** Revenue ₹40,475 cr (+11%), EBITDA ₹21,879 cr (+16.6%, margin 54.1%), PAT ₹8,387 cr (+13.7%). Net adds 9.1 mn. Of the ₹30,188 cr IPO, ₹27,500 cr repays foreign-currency loans, which cuts interest cost.

**What the price assumes.** P/E 41.5 vs Airtel's 39.2, but EV/EBITDA 17.6 vs Airtel's 11.0, so Jio carries a premium on cash earnings. Scenarios (E): bear ₹1,086 (10% profit growth, 25x), base ₹1,588 (15%, 32x), bull ₹2,143 (20%, 38x) against ₹1,406 today. The swing factor is tariff hikes, which are a policy and competition call, not a Jio call.

### 2. NSE: the market's operating system

**Value chain.** Companies raising money → **exchange: NSE (matching, listing, Nifty indices, data)** → clearing corporation (NSE Clearing, owned by NSE) → depositories (NSDL, CDSL) → brokers (Zerodha, Groww, Angel One) → 13.7 crore registered investors. NSE owns the two layers every trade must pass through, which is why it keeps a 76% EBITDA margin while brokers fight over price.

**Why it leads.** Liquidity attracts liquidity: a trader goes where the tightest prices are, and the tightest prices are where everyone else already is. NSE also owns the Nifty brand, the reference index for options, ETFs and index funds.

**Second-order effects.**

- Weekly Nifty options alone were 37.31% of Q2 income. Any SEBI rule on retail F&O hits NSE first; FY26 profit fell 15% after the earlier curbs.
- BSE's Sensex options have taken about a third of options premium (NSE is at 68%). Exchange competition is now real in one product, not in the others.
- The IPO boom is a free option for NSE: listing income rose 70% and NSE handled 81% of mainboard issues in Q2.

| Product | For a user | For an investor: how NSE earns | For a business or partner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equity cash market | Buy and sell shares | Transaction fee per trade; ₹474 cr in Q2 (+30%); 93% share | Brokers route orders here by default |
| Equity derivatives (Nifty, stock options, futures) | Hedge a portfolio or take a leveraged view | ₹1,699 cr from weekly Nifty options alone; ~100% of futures | Funds hedge; market makers earn spreads |
| Listing services | A company lists and raises capital | Listing and annual fees; ₹150 cr (+70%) | Merchant bankers, IPO-bound firms |
| Indices and data (NSE Indices, data feeds) | Your index fund tracks the Nifty 50 | Licence and data fees; data feeds ₹165 cr (+43%) | AMCs and ETF issuers pay to use the Nifty name |
| Clearing (NSE Clearing) | Your trade settles even if the other side defaults | Clearing fees; ₹73 cr (+32%) | Brokers post margin here |
| New lines: MF platform, commodities, electricity futures, coal exchange | Buy mutual funds via your broker; trade power | Small today; MF orders up 155% but only ₹13 cr revenue | Utilities hedge power prices (64% share) |

**Q2 FY27.** Revenue ₹4,067 cr (+11%), PAT ₹2,578 cr (+23% reported). Strip out last year's ₹1,297 cr SEBI settlement fee and ₹1,201 cr one-off gain and pre-tax profit grew 9%. Profit fell 17% from Q1 as options activity cooled.

{{< chart src="n05-nse-income.svg" label="Figure 6" source="IPO Central, based on NSE's 3 Nov 2026 investor presentation" >}}

**What the price assumes.** P/E 46.2 vs BSE's 50.3, on a business growing 9-11% underlying. Scenarios (E): bear ₹1,487 (6% growth, 30x), base ₹2,163 (11%, 38x), bull ₹2,923 (16%, 45x) against ₹1,908. The asymmetric risk is regulatory, not competitive.

### 3. SBI Funds Management: the cheapest distribution in Indian savings

**Value chain.** Savers → distributors (23,265 SBI branches, 1.36 lakh partners, apps like Groww) → **asset manager: SBI Funds (product design, investing)** → registrars and custodians → markets. The AMC keeps about 36 bps a year on mutual-fund assets after paying distributors. SBI's edge is that its parent bank is the distributor, so gathering assets costs it less than rivals.

**Why it leads.** Largest AMC by quarterly average AUM: ₹13.14 lakh cr in mutual funds, 15.0% of the industry, and 27.4% of all passive (index and ETF) money. It reaches 98% of India's postal codes, and 65.5% of its SIP money comes from beyond the top 30 cities.

**Second-order effects.**

- AUM share overstates revenue share: passive money (31.6% of its MF book) earns a fraction of an active fund's fee.
- Share in active equity is slipping (12.53%, down 12 bps in the quarter) and its share of new SIPs fell to 9.94%. Direct apps, including Groww's own AMC, are taking the youngest investors.
- It is a geared bet on Indian markets: a 10% market rise lifts fee income almost automatically, and a fall does the reverse.

| Product | For a saver | For an investor: how SBI Funds earns | For a business or partner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Active equity funds | A professionally run stock portfolio | Highest-fee AUM; ₹8.99 lakh cr active MF assets, 12.4% share | Distributors earn trail commission |
| SIPs | Invest a fixed sum every month | Sticky flows: ₹2.07 lakh cr SIP AUM, 1.62 cr live SIPs | Banks and apps earn on each SIP |
| Passive funds (ETFs, index funds) | Cheap market exposure | Low fee, huge scale: ₹4.15 lakh cr, 27.4% share | Large institutions park money here |
| Debt and liquid funds | Park spare cash for better than a savings account | Low fee, volatile flows | Corporate treasuries |
| PMS and advisory | Managed accounts and mandates | ₹13.3 lakh cr, mostly low-fee advisory; down 17% on one mandate | Institutions and pension pools |
| AIF and SIF (e.g. Magnum Equity Ex-Top 100 Long Short) | Long-short and alternative strategies for richer investors | Higher fees: SIF ₹4,400 cr (16% share), AIF ₹7,400 cr | Wealth managers and family offices |

**Q2 FY27.** Revenue ₹1,193 cr (+9%), PAT ₹701 cr (-6%), as operating costs rose 24% on cloud, AI and hiring. Management says 60% of the higher cost will recur. The stock hit ₹433.55 after results, 24% below its ₹574 issue price.

{{< chart src="n06-sbi-costs.svg" label="Figure 7" source="SBI Funds Q2 FY27 slides via Investing.com; Business Standard" >}}

**What the price assumes.** P/E 33.7 vs HDFC AMC's 38.2, with 43% ROE and 82% operating margin in FY26. Scenarios (E): bear ₹474 (8% growth, 25x), base ₹695 (13%, 32x), bull ₹940 (18%, 38x) against ₹500. Brokers after results: Equirus ₹540, Motilal Oswal ₹590. The market is pricing the cost step-up as permanent and the distribution moat as fading; the first is half-true, the second is not yet visible in AUM.

### 4. Groww: the front door to Indian markets

**Value chain.** Exchanges and AMCs (NSE, BSE, fund houses) → clearing and depositories → **platform: Groww (app, broking, margin lending, wealth)** → 2.4 crore transacting users. Groww owns the customer, pays the exchange its fee and keeps the rest: brokerage, interest on margin loans and float on client cash. EBITDA margin was 65.7% in Q2.

**Why it leads.** Largest broker by active NSE clients, built on the lowest cost to acquire users in Indian finance: growth spend fell to 7.0% of revenue (from 12.1%) while users grew 27%. It started with commission-free direct mutual funds, so users arrive as savers and graduate to traders and borrowers.

**Second-order effects.**

- Groww is now taking flows from AMCs' own channels: it carries 14.8% of India's SIP inflows and runs its own AMC (₹7,179 cr AUM, +145%). That is a direct threat to SBI Funds' share of new SIPs.
- Derivatives are still 44.8% of income. SEBI's Closing Auction System (from 3 Aug 2026) cut F&O revenue 14.3% in one quarter; one rule can move the P&L.
- Lending (margin loans, personal loans, loans against shares) is the diversifier, but it eats cash: operating cash flow was negative in FY25 and FY26 as these books grew.

| Product | For a user | For an investor: how Groww earns | For a business or partner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stocks | Buy shares in a few taps | Brokerage; 17.4% of income; retail share 15.4% | Volume for NSE and BSE |
| F&O | Options and futures trading | Brokerage per order; 44.8% of income | Biggest driver of exchange fees |
| Mutual funds and SIPs | Direct funds, no commission | Little direct fee; builds trust and data | AMCs get cheap distribution |
| Margin Trading Facility | Borrow to buy more shares | Interest; book ₹4,351 cr; 10.1% of income | Funded by Groww's balance sheet |
| Personal loans and loans against shares | Credit without selling investments | Interest; 5.9% of income | Partner lenders and NBFC arm |
| Commodities | Trade gold, crude and others | 6.2% of income, from 0.2% a year ago | MCX and NSE commodity volumes |
| Wealth (W, Fisdom) and Groww AMC | Advice, PMS, AIF, insurance; Groww's own funds | Fee income; AMC still loss-making (−₹8.3 cr) | Bank partners via Fisdom |

**Q2 FY27.** Total income ₹1,541 cr (+44%), EBITDA ₹969 cr (+60%), PAT ₹725 cr (+54%). Customer assets ₹3.9 lakh cr (+46%). 42 lakh users applied for IPOs through Groww in the quarter.

{{< chart src="n07-groww-mix.svg" label="Figure 8" source="Groww Q2 FY27 shareholder letter via Multibagg" >}}

**What the price assumes.** P/E 50.1 vs Angel One's 26.3; the premium buys a cheaper user engine and a broader revenue mix. Scenarios (E): bear ₹126 (15% growth, 25x), base ₹227 (25%, 35x), bull ₹367 (35%, 45x) against ₹215. The bear case is a regulatory hit to F&O, not a competitor.

### 5. LG Electronics India: the brand in the Indian kitchen

**Value chain.** Components (compressors, display panels, chips, much of it from Korea and China) and parent technology → **assembly: LG's Noida and Pune plants, Sri City next** → **brand and distribution: dealers, large-format stores, e-commerce** → after-sales service → households. LG captures the brand and distribution layer, but competition and imported inputs cap the EBITDA margin near 10%.

**Why it leads.** #1 offline market share in washing machines, refrigerators, panel TVs, inverter ACs and microwaves (DRHP). The moat is trust built over nearly three decades plus a service network that rivals cannot copy quickly. Note the word offline: online, the brand advantage is thinner.

**Second-order effects.**

- Low penetration of ACs and refrigerators gives a long runway, but every heatwave summer pulls sales into one quarter (Q4 FY26 revenue ₹8,054 cr vs Q3's ₹4,114 cr).
- The parent holds 85%, sets technology access and earns royalties. Minority holders are passengers on LG Korea's India strategy.
- In-vehicle infotainment from Sri City puts LG into the auto component chain, where it sells to carmakers rather than households.

| Product | For a household | For an investor: how LG India earns | For a business or partner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home appliances and air solutions (refrigerators, washing machines, ACs, microwaves, water purifiers) | Durable, well-serviced appliances | ₹4,772 cr in Q2 (+20.9%); 65% of revenue | Dealers earn on volume and service |
| Home entertainment (OLED and LED TVs, monitors, audio) | Premium screens | ₹2,608 cr in Q2 (+17.2%) | Retail chains use LG as a premium anchor |
| Vehicle solutions (in-vehicle infotainment) | Screens and systems in your next car | New; needs shareholder approval | Carmakers looking for local suppliers |
| After-sales service | Repairs and installation | Supports price premium more than profit | Service franchisees |

**Q2 FY27.** Revenue ₹7,380 cr (+19.5%), EBITDA ₹701 cr (+28%, margin 9.5%), PAT ₹489 cr (+25.7%). A strong quarter on a weak base: FY26 profit had fallen 24%.

**What the price assumes.** P/E 61.9 vs Whirlpool India's 38.4, on 10% five-year sales growth and 2% five-year profit growth. Return ratios are excellent (ROCE 32%). Scenarios (E): bear ₹1,094 (8% growth, 35x), base ₹1,654 (14%, 45x), bull ₹2,358 (20%, 55x) against ₹1,754. Even the base case needs the market to keep paying 45x for a 10%-margin manufacturer.

### 6. Lenskart: the only leader that owns the whole chain

**Value chain.** Frame materials and lens blanks → **manufacturing: own frame plants in India, China and Thailand, in-house lens labs** → **eye test: 80 lakh tests a quarter** → **retail: 2,868 India stores plus app** → customer. In traditional eyewear the margin sits with the neighbourhood optician and the global lens makers; Lenskart takes both by making the product and owning the prescription.

**Why it leads.** Largest eyewear retailer in India. The eye test is the funnel: once Lenskart holds your prescription, the repeat purchase is one tap. Same-store sales grew 20.8% while it added 143 stores in one quarter, so new stores are not cannibalising old ones.

**Second-order effects.**

- Independent opticians lose first; then global lens brands, as in-house lens output rose 52% to 20 lakh units a quarter.
- Management talks of a path to 10,000 stores. That makes Lenskart one of India's biggest buyers of small-format retail space outside metros.
- International (Owndays in Japan and other markets) is already 42% of revenue, so this is partly a global retailer priced as an Indian consumer story.

| Product | For a customer | For an investor: how Lenskart earns | For a business or partner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eyeglasses (own brands such as Vincent Chase, John Jacobs, Lenskart Air) | Frames plus prescription lenses at fixed prices | Highest margin: own frame, own lens, own store | Franchise partners in smaller towns |
| Eye tests (in store and at home) | Free or low-cost vision check | Customer acquisition; 80 lakh tests in Q2 (+40.4%) | Optometrists employed at scale |
| Sunglasses and contact lenses | Fashion and convenience | Higher purchase frequency | Brand collaborations |
| Membership and app | Discounts, repeat orders, virtual try-on | Repeat revenue and customer data | Not applicable |
| International (Owndays and others) | Same model in Japan and Asia | ₹1,201 cr revenue in Q2 (+29.5%), EBITDA ₹264 cr | Shares the India supply chain |

**Q2 FY27.** Revenue ₹2,845 cr (+32.5%), EBITDA ₹624 cr (+46.6%, margin 21.9%), PAT ₹232 cr (more than doubled). India revenue ₹1,668 cr (+35.3%). Eyewear units sold 1.1 cr (+27.1%). Cash ₹3,855 cr; company-reported ROCE 22.5%.

**What the price assumes.** P/E 161 and EV/EBITDA 52.8, the highest on this list. Scenarios (E), built on FY29 profit of ₹1,400 / 1,800 / 2,200 cr at 50x / 65x / 80x: bear ₹402, base ₹673, bull ₹1,012 against ₹725. The business is executing; the base case still returns less than today's price because the next three years of growth are already paid for.

### 7. Urban Company: a good core business funding an expensive experiment

**Value chain.** Independent professionals (beauticians, cleaners, electricians, plumbers) → training, tools and consumables (sold by Urban Company to the pros) → **platform: Urban Company (matching, pricing, quality control)** → households. Plus Native, its own hardware brand. The platform keeps a service fee; the core India business earned 3.5% of transaction value as adjusted EBITDA in Q2, against a management target of 9-10%.

**Why it leads.** The largest organised home-services platform. The moat is local supply density: a trained, rated professional available in your neighbourhood within hours. Management says penetration of its largest categories is still below 10% in the top eight cities.

**Second-order effects.**

- InstaHelp (15-minute house help) is the swing factor. Orders rose to 6.23 mn in Q2 and the loss per order fell to ₹279, but total loss rose to ₹174 cr because orders grew faster. It competes with informal maids, which means its real price ceiling is set by the local labour market.
- Profitable parts (India core, international, Native combined) made about ₹30 cr; InstaHelp lost ₹174 cr. Shareholders are funding the new bet with the old one's profits.
- The board wants approval to raise up to ₹2,500 cr; at ₹158 that is about 9.3% dilution.

| Product | For a household | For a professional | For an investor: how UC earns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home services (salon at home, cleaning, repairs, appliance service, painting) | Vetted pro at a fixed price | Steady jobs, training, tools | Service fee plus product sales; core India adjusted EBITDA ₹34 cr in Q2 |
| InstaHelp | House help in about 15 minutes | Short paid shifts | Loss-making: ₹114 per order today vs ₹300 needed |
| Native (water purifiers, smart locks) | Low-maintenance branded hardware | Installation and service jobs | Product revenue, ~₹400 cr a year run rate; still loss-making |
| International (UAE, Singapore; Saudi JV) | Same service abroad | Same | Small profit (₹5 cr in Q2) |

**Q2 FY27.** Revenue ₹537 cr (+41%), net loss ₹182.5 cr (from ₹59 cr). Core India transaction value grew 26%. Cash and treasury ₹1,901 cr.

{{< chart src="n09-urban-split.svg" label="Figure 10" source="Urban Company Q2 FY27 results; Dhruva Pandey" >}}

**What the price assumes.** Loss-making, so it trades on sales: 13x. Scenarios (E) on FY29 revenue growth of 30% / 38% / 45% a year at 5x / 8x / 11x sales, plus cash: bear ₹123, base ₹225, bull ₹351 against ₹158. A detailed independent [cash-flow model](https://dhruva.substack.com/p/urban-company-q2-fy27-the-core-is) finds ₹158 needs about 34% annual core growth for ten years for a 15% return. The base case works only if InstaHelp reaches break-even on schedule (FY31).

### 8. Waaree Energies: moving upstream before the module glut arrives

**Value chain.** Polysilicon → ingots and wafers (mostly China) → **cells: Waaree adding 10 GW in India this year, 15.4 GW by FY28** → **modules: largest Indian maker, 29 GW of PV capacity planned by FY28, plus a US plant in Texas** → EPC and project developers → utilities, factories and rooftops. Profit in solar sits wherever supply is scarcest. Today that is cells in India (the government's approved-list rules now push developers to Indian cells) and local manufacturing in the US (tariffs and production credits). Waaree is building into both.

**Why it leads.** Largest module capacity in India, a ₹59,200 cr order book, and a retail network of 775+ channels and 3,000+ distribution points that most rivals lack.

**Second-order effects.**

- US Section 232 import pricing made India-to-US exports risky: Waaree dropped 3 GW+ of export orders from its book and exports fell to 7.8% of the order book (from about 24%). Exports from India brought in ₹89 cr in Q2 vs ₹2,143 cr a year ago.
- The shift pays: management says US-made modules earn about 8-9 cents per watt (including a 7-cent production incentive) vs 3-4 cents on India exports.
- Indian module capacity is growing faster than demand. The winners will be makers with their own cells; module-only assemblers are the likely losers.

| Product | For a buyer | For an investor: how Waaree earns | For a business or partner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Utility-scale solar modules | Panels for solar farms | Bulk of revenue; module order book 25.6 GW | Utility-scale developers and government tenders |
| Solar cells | The power-generating core inside a panel | Higher margin as rules favour Indian cells | Sold to other module makers |
| Rooftop and retail kits | Solar for a home or shop | H1 retail revenue ₹5,753 cr, 37% of total; FY27 target ₹10,000-11,000 cr | Local installers and dealers |
| US-made modules (Brookshire, Texas) | Tariff-proof panels for US projects | 1.6 GW live, 4.8 GW planned; earns US incentives | US developers needing local content |
| Battery storage (packs and cells, planned) | Stores solar power for evening use | Planned 5 GWh packs, 3.5 GWh cells by FY28 | Utilities and C&I buyers |

**Q2 FY27.** Revenue ₹7,518 cr (+24%), PAT ₹754 cr (down from ₹878 cr), operating margin 18% vs 23% a year ago. FY27 EBITDA guidance of ₹7,000-7,700 cr was kept, with 55-60% expected in the second half. Free cash flow was −₹3,209 cr in FY26 because of a ₹30,000 cr capex programme.

{{< chart src="n10-waaree.svg" label="Figure 11" source="Waaree Q2 FY27 concall via Multibagg" >}}

**What the price assumes.** P/E 18 and EV/EBITDA 10.0 (about 9.5x guided FY27 EBITDA) vs Premier Energies at 22.8x earnings. The stock is 31% below its 52-week high. Scenarios (E): bear ₹1,180 (profit falls 10% a year, 12x), base ₹2,811 (5% growth, 18x), bull ₹4,923 (15%, 24x) against ₹2,438. Cheapest on paper; the widest range of outcomes on the list.

## Value-chain map

The profit pool sits at the layer every transaction must pass through: the exchange for NSE, the network for Jio, the customer's app for Groww and Urban Company. Manufacturers keep less because their inputs are bought from someone else and their output competes on price.

{{< chart src="n12-value-chain.svg" label="Figure 12" source="My classification from the company sections above" >}}

Three patterns fall out of the map:

- **Rails beat makers on margin, makers beat rails on reinvestment.** NSE (76% EBITDA margin) needs little capital; Waaree needs ₹30,000 cr of capex to stay ahead. The market pays 46x for the first and 18x for the second, which is rational only if the maker's growth is cyclical.
- **Customer owners collide with each other.** Groww's AMC and 14.8% share of SIP inflows go straight at SBI Funds' new-investor share. Watch these two as a pair: one's distribution win is the other's flow loss.
- **Full-stack ownership is rare and priced in.** Lenskart is the only leader that controls making, distributing and the customer relationship. That explains both its 22% EBITDA margin and its 161x P/E.

## Risks and what would change the view

Three of the eight leaders (NSE, Groww, SBI Funds) are one bet on Indian retail market activity, and two of those carry direct SEBI rule risk. Holding all three is less diversified than it looks.

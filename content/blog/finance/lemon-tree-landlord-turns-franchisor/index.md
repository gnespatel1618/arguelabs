**Thesis in one line:** Lemon Tree is splitting into a debt-free brand company and a listed landlord, and at Rs 124.8 the market already pays ~30x for the brand half. The stock works only if third-party fees keep compounding at 20%+ through a tier 3 supply wave, and Fleur, its biggest fee payer, keeps paying the same fees after it lists.

The business is good and getting better: record Q2 FY27 with RevPAR +18%, occupancy 76%, third-party fees +30%, debt down to ~2x EBITDA. But ~80% of EBITDA still comes from buildings, occupancy is near its ceiling, and management's Rs 500 Cr fee goal needs the entire current pipeline opened and seasoned, which is an FY31–FY32 outcome. Probability-weighted value is ~Rs 123, about today's price.

## What Lemon Tree sells

Lemon Tree sells a hotel room to the Indian middle class at five price points, and increasingly sells its brand to other people's buildings. At 30 Sep 2026 it ran 138 hotels with 12,131 rooms in 80+ cities. Of those rooms, 5,679 sit in 41 owned or leased hotels and 6,452 (up 24% YoY) are managed or franchised for third parties ([Q2 FY27 slides](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/lemon-tree-hotels-q2-fy27-slides-record-quarter-drives-90-profit-surge-93CH-5018608)).

### Five brands, one ladder

| Brand | Segment | Who it is for | Q2 FY27 RevPAR growth | Owned-hotel EBITDAR margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aurika | Upper upscale / luxury | Weddings, MICE, airport and leisure; flagship is the 669-room Aurika Mumbai Skycity at T2 | +17% | 59% |
| Lemon Tree Premier | Upscale | Business travel in metros; LTP Delhi does north of Rs 10,000 ARR | +18% | 56% |
| Lemon Tree Hotels | Upper midscale | The core: corporate and family travel in tier 1–3 cities | +15% | n.d. |
| Red Fox | Midscale / economy | Price-sensitive business travellers | +21% | n.d. |
| Keys (Prima, Select, Lite) | Economy to midscale | Bought with Berggruen Hotels in 2019; now the franchise workhorse for small towns | +29% | 50% |

The top of the ladder carries the profit. Aurika and Lemon Tree Premier earned 63% of owned-hotel EBITDAR on 58% of revenue in Q2 FY27, and Mumbai alone earned 30% of EBITDAR on 26% of revenue ([Hotelier India](https://www.hotelierindia.com/operations/lemon-tree-hotels-reports-90-growth-in-q2-fy27-pat-to-%E2%82%B979-8-crore)). The bottom of the ladder carries the growth: most new signings are Lemon Tree, Keys and Red Fox hotels in tier 2–4 towns, run for owners who supply the capital.

### Three revenue lines

1. **Room nights in owned hotels.** Rs 337 Cr in Q2 FY27, 93% of consolidated revenue. Rooms plus F&B, banquets and weddings. This is a real-estate business with an operator attached.
2. **Fees from Fleur.** Rs 22.1 Cr in Q2 (Rs 95.8 Cr in FY26). Today these net out inside the consolidated accounts because Fleur is a subsidiary. After the demerger they become visible revenue.
3. **Fees from third-party owners.** Rs 18.7 Cr in Q2, up 30% (Rs 73.9 Cr in FY26, up 23%). Base fee on revenue plus an incentive fee on gross operating profit. This is the line the whole strategy is built to grow.

The network is bigger than the P&L shows. Revenue across all hotels flying a Lemon Tree flag was Rs 573 Cr in Q2, up 23%, of which owned hotels were 59%. The other 41% is revenue Lemon Tree touches but books only as a fee.

### What is coming

The signed pipeline is 147 hotels and 11,930 rooms, about the size of the whole operating portfolio today. On top sits an MoU with Omaxe (16 Oct 2026) to operate 10 hotels with about 4,000 keys, which would take the pipeline to roughly 16,000 keys ([DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/lemon-tree-hotels-signs-4000-key-operating-pact-with-omaxe-lifting-pipeline-towards-16000-keys)). Owned Aurika projects in Fleur: Shimla (90 rooms, FY27), Shillong (165, FY28, a JV), Varanasi (47, FY29), a 172-room Lemon Tree Premier on Rs 120 Cr of land in Bandra East (FY30) and a 610-room Aurika at Nehru Place, Delhi (now CY31, waiting on DDA and Ridge Board approvals).

## Two businesses in one stock

Today Lemon Tree is mostly a landlord wearing an operator's badge: owned hotels are 93% of revenue. The composite scheme splits it into a debt-free fee company (Lemon Tree) and a listed asset owner (Fleur Hotels). Boards approved it on 9 Jan 2026, the appointed date is 1 Apr 2026, CCI cleared it in April, and it now sits with NCLT. Fleur's listing is targeted for calendar 2027 ([Warburg Pincus release](https://warburgpincus.com/2026/01/09/lemon-tree-hotels-announces-strategic-reorganization-and-investment-from-warburg-pincus-in-fleur-hotels-to-unlock-long-term-shareholder-value/)).

{{< chart src="l01-scheme-structure.svg" label="Figure 1" source="Warburg Pincus release (9 Jan 2026), Q4 FY26 call, Q2 FY27 slides" >}}

For every 311 Lemon Tree shares you hold, you get 20 Fleur shares ([ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/research/equity/trending-news/lemon-tree-hotels-restructures-into-fleur-hotels)). Counting the 41% Lemon Tree keeps, your look-through stake in Fleur rises from about 59% to about 74%, before Warburg's up-to-Rs 960 Cr primary cheque dilutes it. Warburg bought APG's 41% on the way in, so the old JV partner is out.

### The fee machine, from first principles

Management's stated goal is Rs 500 Cr of fees and Rs 400 Cr of EBITDA for standalone Lemon Tree, with no date attached. Trailing fees (Oct 2025–Sep 2026) are about Rs 184 Cr: ~Rs 99 Cr from Fleur and ~Rs 85 Cr from third parties. So the target is 2.7x today.

The captive book will not get there. Fleur fees grow 8–11% a year because those hotels are mature. If Fleur fees reach ~Rs 170 Cr, third-party fees must reach ~Rs 330 Cr, about 4x today. Third parties pay roughly Rs 1.4–1.8 lakh a room a year once stabilised. That needs about 19,000–23,000 stabilised third-party rooms.

That is almost exactly 6,452 operating + ~10,900 pipeline + ~4,000 Omaxe. **The Rs 500 Cr target is the whole current pipeline, opened and seasoned.** At management's rough 2,000 / 3,000 / 4,000 rooms a year for FY27–FY29, plus two to three years to stabilise, that is an FY31–FY32 number, not FY28.

### The second-order catch

The fee company's best customer becomes a separate listed company with a 26% private-equity shareholder. Fleur's deck says it will have "strategic flexibility" to work with other brands. Today Fleur pays 54% of Lemon Tree's fees. Any renegotiation of that contract at listing is the single biggest swing factor for the asset-light story. Elara's view is blunter: the scheme gave APG an exit but creates little value, because Lemon Tree's 41% Fleur stake will trade at a holding-company discount.

## Unit economics: occupancy is nearly full, so rate must do the work

RevPAR has risen about 23% in two years, and margins have not followed. Net EBITDA margin slipped from 49.4% in FY25 to 48.1% in FY26 and was 44.6% in Q2 FY27, because renovation, the September 2025 GST change and stock-appreciation-rights provisions took about 3–6% of revenue.

### Four things the table hides

1. **The occupancy lever is mostly pulled.** 76% in a seasonally soft quarter is close to what a city-hotel portfolio can sustain. Q2 FY27's 18% RevPAR growth was two-thirds occupancy. From here, growth has to come from ARR, and management says corporate demand (IT hiring in Gurgaon especially) is still muted.
2. **Mumbai is the swing asset.** The 669-room Aurika Mumbai Skycity opened in late 2023 and lifted Aurika's brand occupancy from 62% to 74% in FY26. Mumbai now earns 30% of owned-hotel EBITDAR. But ~2,000 new keys arrived in its micro-market, and a wet-lease crew contract worth ~8% of occupancy ended in Q4 FY26. Aurika's Q1 FY26 margin was 58%; Q2 FY27 was 59%.
3. **The margin drag is temporary, by management's own numbers.** Renovation + GST + technology cost 5.8% of revenue in FY26, guided to 4.8% in FY27 and 3.7% in FY28. About 85% of the 4,000 heavy-renovation rooms are done and Keys is ~80% refurbished. Management guides FY28 net EBITDA margin of at least 50%.
4. **The Q2 profit headline is flattered.** PAT rose 90% to Rs 79.8 Cr, but that includes a Rs 41.6 Cr gain on terminating the lease of Lemon Tree Premier 2, Gurugram. PBT before exceptional items rose 59% to Rs 88.8 Cr, which is the cleaner read ([ScanX](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/companies/lemon-tree-hotels-q2fy27-results-net-profit-up-90-yoy-7-976-lakh/55444599)).

The brand mix explains why the operator wants to move up the ladder. A Keys room earns a 50% EBITDAR margin at a fraction of Aurika's ARR (Aurika ran ~Rs 10,900 ARR in Q3 FY26). An owned Aurika room at a 59% margin generates several times the cash of a Keys room, which is why Fleur's new money goes to Aurika and Premier projects while the economy brands go to franchisees.

## Balance sheet and capital allocation

The deleveraging is done; the re-leveraging moves to Fleur. Gross debt fell from Rs 1,889 Cr (FY24) to Rs 1,429 Cr (Sep 2026), debt/EBITDA from 3.57x to about 2x, and cost of debt from 8.86% to 7.44% ([India Infoline](https://www.indiainfoline.com/news/business/lemon-tree-hotels-q4-profit-jumps-26-yoy-to-84-64-crore), [Hotelier India](https://www.hotelierindia.com/operations/lemon-tree-hotels-reports-90-growth-in-q2-fy27-pat-to-%E2%82%B979-8-crore)). After the scheme, all of it sits in Fleur and standalone Lemon Tree has none.

### Where the scheme stands

1. 9 Jan 2026: boards approve; Warburg agrees to buy APG's 41.09% of Fleur and commits up to Rs 960 Cr of primary equity.
2. 1 Apr 2026: appointed date. From Q1 FY27 the company shows pro forma numbers for each half: Lemon Tree revenue +22% YoY, Fleur +7% in Q1.
3. Apr–May 2026: CCI approval and detailed order.
4. Now: with NCLT; shareholder and creditor meetings next.
5. Calendar 2027: Fleur lists on NSE and BSE (management's outer range was 12–18 months from January 2026).

### What Fleur will do with the money

Fleur plans about Rs 3,000 Cr of capex and acquisitions over three and a half years. Management says it will take Warburg's money "if and only if we can deploy it", pays 8–16x EBITDA for existing hotels, and spends about Rs 2 Cr a key on greenfield. The named projects are Bandra East (172 rooms, Rs 120 Cr land, FY30, target 12–13% ROCE at ~Rs 12,000 opening ARR) and Aurika Nehru Place (~610 rooms, ~Rs 700 Cr, now CY31). Fleur targets roughly 2,500 more owned rooms by FY30–31.

A 12–13% ROCE is about the cost of equity, so Fleur's growth capex is roughly value-neutral on its own. Its value to Lemon Tree shareholders is the fee stream each new owned room adds to the asset-light half, plus the 74% look-through stake.

### What standalone Lemon Tree will do with its cash

Undecided. Management calls it a "zero-debt cash accretive company" and says the board will set a dividend or buyback policy later. Non-core bets like F&B face a "very high hurdle rate". A fee business with ~Rs 100 Cr of overhead and almost no capex should pay out most of its profit; whether it does is worth watching at listing.

## Future growth: the pipeline is the business

Lemon Tree's growth from here is mostly other people's capital. The company projects ~26,400 operating rooms by FY31, more than double today, and almost all the increase is managed or franchised.

The slope steepens late. H1 FY27 added only ~320 net rooms (11,811 to 12,131) because ~530 non-compliant franchise rooms were de-flagged; management says none are due in H2 and 900+ openings are lined up. The plan needs 3,000–4,000 openings a year from FY28, against 1,523 in FY26.

### Six growth levers, in order of how much they matter

1. **Third-party room count.** 6,452 operating, ~10,900 signed, ~4,000 under the Omaxe MoU. FY26 signings were 4,912 rooms in 55 hotels, so the funnel refills faster than it empties.
2. **Fee per room as hotels season.** A new hotel's fees rise 50–60% once it stabilises, because incentive fees kick in on GOP. Third-party fees grew 36% in H1 FY27 against 24% room growth.
3. **ARR at the owned hotels.** Occupancy is ~76%, so rate is the lever: Mumbai, Delhi and Pune carry it, and renovated Keys hotels posted +29% RevPAR in Q2.
4. **Margin recovery.** The cost drag falls from 5.8% to 3.7% of revenue by FY28, worth ~2 points of margin on its own; guidance is ≥50% net EBITDA margin in FY28.
5. **New owned premium hotels through Fleur.** Shimla, Shillong, Varanasi, Bandra and Nehru Place add ~1,080 premium rooms by CY31, each paying fees to Lemon Tree.
6. **International.** Hotels in Bhutan, Nepal and Dubai, plus an 85-room Nepal licence. Small; optionality only.

### The industry backdrop has changed

The "demand beats supply" story that re-rated Indian hotels in 2022–25 has stalled at the national level. In FY26 branded supply grew 5.8% and occupied room nights 5.6%; all-India occupancy was flat at 68.1% and RevPAR rose 6.2%, all on rate ([Hotelivate 2026](https://www.hotelivate.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TO-2026.pdf)).

The pipeline is 161,074 rooms, 77% of today's branded stock. Tier 3 cities hold 49% of it, and over 70% of FY26 additions were budget to upper-midmarket. That is precisely where Lemon Tree franchises.

The second-order effect cuts both ways:

- **Good for the fee business.** Thousands of first-time owners in small towns need a brand, a booking engine and a manager. Lemon Tree, sixth in India by inventory, is one of few Indian midscale brands with a 20-year track record. Signings should stay strong.
- **Bad for fee quality.** New supply in tier 3 towns pressures RevPAR and GOP at exactly the hotels Lemon Tree manages, and 30–40% of management fees track GOP. The 530 rooms de-flagged in H1 show the quality risk of franchising fast in small towns.
- **Neutral to good for owned hotels.** Fleur's assets sit mainly in Delhi, Mumbai, Pune, Bengaluru and Hyderabad, where supply growth is lower (metros ~5.4% CAGR FY25–30 per Yes Securities, via [The Core](https://www.thecore.in/business/india-top-tier-luxury-hotel-travel-2026-856023)).

## Competition and peers

Lemon Tree trades below most listed Indian hotel peers because the market prices it as an owner of mid-market buildings, not as a brand. On MOFSL's April 2026 numbers it traded at 12.5x FY27E EV/EBITDA against 15–20x for the upscale owners and IHCL.

### Who Lemon Tree really competes with

There are two fights, and they have different winners.

- **For guests in owned hotels:** mid-market and upscale hotels in Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Pune, Bengaluru and Hyderabad. Here location and rate discipline matter more than brand. Lemon Tree runs occupancy 400–600 bps above the national average.
- **For owners' signatures:** IHCL (Ginger, plus majority stakes in Clarks and Brij), Marriott (now with a minority stake in Concept Hospitality and the Series by Marriott brand), IHG, Hyatt, Radisson, Wyndham and domestic chains like Sarovar and Royal Orchid. Marriott leads India by operating inventory, IHCL by operating hotels; Lemon Tree is sixth ([Hotelivate 2026](https://www.hotelivate.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TO-2026.pdf)).

The owner fight is the one that matters for the fee thesis. A small-town owner chooses on three things: cost of the brand, the share of bookings the brand delivers, and the brand's pull with corporate travel desks. Lemon Tree's edge is that it was built for this segment, with a 4–6% fee and 20 years of mid-market distribution. Its weakness is that global brands now chase the same owners with cheaper conversion brands, and the top 10 brands already hold ~67% of branded supply.

## Valuation: the price already assumes the base case

At Rs 124.8 the market values the whole group at ~Rs 9,890 Cr, which is roughly what a sum of the parts gives today. If Fleur is worth 15–17x trailing EBITDA, the market is paying 29–35x for the fee business, about what MOFSL and global asset-light operators command. There is no obvious mispricing; the return comes from the fee line compounding.

### Splitting the trailing numbers (Oct 2025–Sep 2026)

The company has not published a rupee split, so these are estimates built from disclosed fees and management's flow-through figures:

- **Group:** revenue ~Rs 1,540 Cr, net EBITDA ~Rs 738 Cr.
- **Lemon Tree standalone:** fees ~Rs 184 Cr at ~60% flow-through = ~Rs 110 Cr, plus ~Rs 30 Cr from the two leased hotels = **~Rs 140 Cr EBITDA**.
- **Fleur:** the remainder, **~Rs 600 Cr EBITDA after fees**, with ~Rs 1,400 Cr of net debt.
- **Look-through:** a Lemon Tree share carries 0.0643 Fleur shares directly and ~0.080 through Lemon Tree's 41.03% stake (74% in total, before Warburg's primary).

At 15x, Fleur's equity is ~Rs 7,600 Cr. Applying a 20% holding-company discount to the indirect stake gives Lemon Tree holders ~Rs 5,000 Cr. That leaves ~Rs 4,890 Cr for the fee business, or ~35x its EBITDA. At 17x for Fleur the fee business is implied at ~29x.

### Kill switches

1. **Third-party fee growth.** Pass: ≥20% YoY every half-year through FY28. Fail: <12% for two quarters running. Now: +30% in Q2 FY27, +36% in H1.
2. **Openings.** Pass: FY27 gross openings ≥1,800 rooms and FY28 ≥2,500. Fail: FY28 below 2,000. Now: ~850 gross in H1 FY27 (implied), 900+ lined up for H2.
3. **De-flagging.** Pass: under ~2% of the franchised base a year. Fail: another 500+ rooms de-flagged in any half. Now: ~530 in H1 FY27, "nothing on paper" for H2.
4. **Margin.** Pass: FY28 net EBITDA margin ≥50% (guidance). Fail: below 47%. Now: 44.6% in Q2, 48.1% in FY26.
5. **The Fleur contract.** Pass: at Fleur's listing, fee terms and tenure for the 39 hotels unchanged and disclosed. Fail: any cut in fee rate, or other brands placed on existing Fleur hotels.
6. **Listing timeline.** Pass: Fleur lists by Dec 2027. Fail: slips past Mar 2028.
7. **Capital return.** Pass: standalone Lemon Tree announces a payout policy by Fleur's listing. Fail: its cash goes into non-core acquisitions.
8. **Omaxe.** Pass: definitive management agreements for ≥2,000 of the ~4,000 keys signed by Q4 FY27. Fail: MoU still an MoU at the FY27 results.

## Risks and what to watch

The biggest risk is not demand; it is that the fee company's largest customer becomes an independent listed company with a PE shareholder.

| Risk | Why it matters | Signal to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fleur renegotiates fees | Fleur pays ~54% of Lemon Tree's fees today | Fleur DRHP / listing documents, CY27 |
| Tier 3 supply wave | 49% of India's pipeline is tier 3; 30–40% of fees track GOP | Third-party fee growth vs room growth each quarter |
| Franchise quality | 530 rooms de-flagged in H1 FY27 | De-flagging disclosure in Q3/Q4 FY27 |

| Policy | GST change cost 1–2% of revenue; aviation and West Asia shocks hit FY26 | GST council; airline capacity |

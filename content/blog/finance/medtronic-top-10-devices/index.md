## 60-second view

Medtronic's top 10 devices fall into three types: sealed implanted electronics (hardest to copy), razor-and-blade disposables (recurring revenue), and capital robots (lock-in). Indian makers already compete in stents, staplers, TAVR and robots, but not in implanted electronics. Prices are what Indian patients pay, from public hospital and news pages; Medtronic does not publish list prices.

## Where each device works in the body

Half of the ten devices work in or on the heart, so the close-up below zooms in on it.

{{< chart src="p03-heart.svg" label="Heart close-up" source="Schematic, drawn from sections 1 to 5" >}}

## 1. Pacemakers (incl. leadless Micra)

A pacemaker is a small battery-powered computer that fires a tiny shock to keep a slow heart beating on time.

{{< chart src="f01-pacemaker.svg" label="Figure 1" source="Pacemaker and Micra, schematic, not to scale" >}}

### How it works

The heart has its own natural pacemaker, a small cluster of cells that sets each beat. When that signal slows or pauses, the patient feels dizzy or faints. A pacemaker takes over the timing. A wire (lead) runs through a vein into the heart and senses every beat. If the heart pauses or runs too slow, the device fires a small pulse to trigger a beat. If the heart is beating fine on its own, it stays quiet.

Micra drops the wire. The whole device, about the size of a large vitamin pill, is pushed up through the groin vein and hooked into the heart wall.

### How it's made

A lithium battery and a custom chip are sealed inside a laser-welded titanium case, so body fluid can never get in. On top sits a plastic header where the lead plugs in. Leads are coiled metal wire insulated with silicone or polyurethane, so they can flex with every heartbeat for years. Everything is built in cleanrooms, tested, then gas-sterilised.

### Price in India

The device alone costs ₹45,000 to ₹1.5 lakh, and the full procedure about ₹2.75 to 3 lakh. A leadless Micra costs about ₹14 lakh ([Yashoda](https://www.yashodahospitals.com/pacemaker-surgery-cost-in-india/)).

### Competition

Abbott (Aveir leadless), Boston Scientific, Biotronik and MicroPort. Indian maker Shree Pacetronix plays at the low end. Medtronic is the global leader and has the most leadless experience.

**Key terms:** *Lead*: the insulated wire from the device to the heart. *Leadless*: a pacemaker that sits inside the heart, with no wire.

## 2. ICDs and CRT devices

An ICD is a pacemaker with a defibrillator inside: it watches for a deadly fast rhythm and shocks the heart back to normal.

{{< chart src="f02-icd-ecg.svg" label="Figure 2" source="Schematic heart-rhythm trace (ECG), not real patient data" >}}

### How it works

Sometimes the heart's lower chambers suddenly quiver instead of pumping. This is ventricular fibrillation: blood stops flowing, and without a reset the person dies within minutes (sudden cardiac death). An ICD sits under the collarbone and watches every beat through its lead. When it sees this rhythm, it delivers a strong internal shock within seconds that resets the heart (Figure 2). Between emergencies it also works as an ordinary pacemaker.

CRT (cardiac resynchronisation therapy) is for a weak, failing heart whose two sides squeeze out of step. A third lead lets the device pace both sides together, so the heart pumps more blood with each beat. CRT-D is CRT plus a defibrillator in one device.

### How it's made

It is built like a pacemaker, but with a bigger battery and high-voltage capacitors that charge up to about 35 joules for the shock. The case is titanium and the leads carry shock coils. It is harder to make than a pacemaker because the electronics must switch very high energy safely.

### Price in India

An ICD procedure costs ₹1.8 to 3.4 lakh, typically about ₹2.8 lakh ([Yashoda](https://www.yashodahospitals.com/implantable-cardioverter-defibrillator-icd-cost-in-india/)). The page does not say whether the device is included. CRT-D packages start at ₹7.55 lakh ([Credihealth](https://credihealth.com/procedures/india/crt-d-cost)).

### Competition

Boston Scientific (which also makes the S-ICD, whose lead lies under the skin instead of inside the heart), Abbott, Biotronik and MicroPort. Medtronic's newer Aurora EV-ICD puts the lead outside the heart, under the breastbone. There is no Indian maker at scale.

**Key terms:** *Defibrillation*: an electric shock that resets a chaotic heart rhythm. *Capacitor*: a part that stores electric charge and releases it all at once. *CRT*: pacing both sides of the heart so they squeeze together.

## 3. TAVR heart valves (Evolut)

TAVR replaces a narrowed aortic valve without opening the chest: a new valve is crimped onto a catheter and opened inside the old one.

{{< chart src="f03-tavr.svg" label="Figure 3" source="TAVR in three steps, schematic, not to scale" >}}

### How it works

The aortic valve is the heart's exit door: it opens to let blood out to the body and shuts so it can't flow back. With age it can stiffen with calcium (aortic stenosis). The heart then strains to push blood through a narrow opening, and the patient gets breathless, has chest pain or faints. The old fix was open-heart surgery.

In TAVR the doctor threads the folded valve up from the groin artery and releases it inside the old valve, which gets pressed against the wall (Figure 3). No chest is opened. Evolut is self-expanding: its metal frame springs open on its own.

### How it's made

The frame is laser-cut from a nickel-titanium (nitinol) tube, a metal that remembers its shape, so it can be squeezed into a catheter and spring back inside the body. The leaflets are cut from pig heart tissue (pericardium), chemically treated so the body does not reject it, then hand-sewn onto the frame. That sewing is slow, skilled work and the main bottleneck.

### Price in India

Imported valves alone cost ₹13 to 23 lakh ([Business Today, Mar 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/industry/pharma/story/karnataka-medtech-firm-unveils-india-made-tavr-heart-valve-system-to-cut-costs-520333-2026-03-12)). The full procedure costs ₹15 to 30 lakh ([Asian Heart Institute, 2024](https://asianheartinstitute.org/blog/tavr-surgery-cost-in-mumbai-india/)).

### Competition

Edwards Lifesciences (SAPIEN, the global leader), Abbott (Navitor), and in India **Meril (Myval)**, a homegrown balloon-expandable valve that competes on price. S3V Vascular is building another Indian TAVR, still preclinical.

**Key terms:** *Aortic stenosis*: a narrowed, stiff aortic valve. *Nitinol*: a nickel-titanium alloy that returns to its set shape. *Self-expanding vs balloon-expandable*: the valve opens by itself (Evolut) or is pushed open by inflating a balloon (SAPIEN, Myval).

## 4. Coronary stents and balloons (Onyx Frontier)

A stent is a tiny metal mesh tube that props open a blocked heart artery after a balloon has pushed the blockage aside.

{{< chart src="f04-stent.svg" label="Figure 4" source="Angioplasty and stenting, schematic, not to scale" >}}

### How it works

The heart muscle is fed by coronary arteries on its surface. Fatty plaque can narrow them, causing chest pain (angina) or, if a clot forms on it, a heart attack. In angioplasty a cardiologist passes a thin wire from the wrist or groin to the blockage, slides a balloon catheter over it and inflates it. The balloon presses the plaque into the artery wall (Figure 4).

The stent, crimped on the balloon, expands with it and stays as scaffolding after the balloon is pulled out. A drug coating (zotarolimus on Medtronic's Onyx) seeps out slowly and stops scar tissue from re-narrowing the artery.

### How it's made

A thin cobalt-chromium tube is laser-cut into a mesh pattern, then electropolished smooth. Onyx uses a wire with a dense platinum-iridium core so it shows up on X-ray. A polymer mixed with the drug is sprayed on in micron-thin layers, then the stent is crimped onto a balloon catheter.

### Price in India

Stent prices are capped by the government. A drug-eluting stent costs at most ₹39,186 and a bare-metal stent ₹10,762, excluding GST, from 1 April 2026 ([NPPA via Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/medical-devices/nppa-revises-coronary-stent-prices-des-at-rs-39186-and-bms-at-rs-10762-from-april-2026-168192)).

### Competition

Abbott (Xience), Boston Scientific (Synergy), and Indian makers Meril, SMT (Sahajanand) and Translumina. Because the cap applies to every brand, a global brand cannot charge more for its name.

**Key terms:** *Angioplasty*: opening a narrowed artery with a balloon. *Drug-eluting stent (DES)*: a stent coated with a drug that stops re-narrowing. *Restenosis*: the artery narrowing again after treatment.

## 5. Pulsed-field ablation for AFib (PulseSelect, Affera)

PFA treats an irregular heartbeat by zapping the faulty heart tissue with very short electric pulses instead of heat or cold.

{{< chart src="f05-pfa.svg" label="Figure 5" source="Left atrium seen from inside, schematic, not to scale" >}}

### How it works

In a healthy heart each beat starts at one natural pacemaker. In atrial fibrillation (AFib), extra electrical signals, mostly from where the lung veins join the left atrium, make the upper chambers quiver instead of squeeze. Blood can pool and clot there, which raises the risk of stroke. AFib affects over 7 million people in India ([Medtronic](https://news.medtronic.com/Medtronic-Introduces-PulseSelect-TM-PFA-Technology-to-address-Indias-Growing-AFib-and-Stroke-Burden)).

Ablation builds an electrical fence around each vein opening so the stray signals can't get out (Figure 5). A catheter goes up from the groin vein into the left atrium and fires microsecond high-voltage pulses that punch holes in those heart-muscle cells so they stop conducting. Heart muscle is more sensitive to this than the nerves or the food pipe nearby, so it is safer and faster than the older methods of burning (radio-frequency) or freezing (cryo).

### How it's made

It has two parts. The first is a console (pulse generator) that stores and releases high-voltage energy with precise timing. The second is a single-use catheter: PulseSelect has a loop of 9 electrodes on its tip, and Affera Sphere-9 has a lattice ball tip that also maps the heart. The catheters are precision-assembled from braided shafts, fine wiring and electrodes. The disposable catheter is where the recurring revenue comes from.

### Price in India

About ₹5 to 6 lakh per patient, against ₹40 to 50 lakh in the US ([Hitavada, Jul 2026](https://www.thehitavada.com/Encyc/2026/7/17/first-in-gmcs-in-india-ssh-doctors-perform-pulsed-field-ablation-procedure-.html)). Older heat-based ablation costs about ₹4 to 5 lakh ([Moolchand](https://blog.moolchandhealthcare.com/ablation-surgery-explained-types-costs-recovery-in-india)). Medtronic launched PulseSelect in India in June 2026 ([Medtronic](https://news.medtronic.com/Medtronic-Introduces-PulseSelect-TM-PFA-Technology-to-address-Indias-Growing-AFib-and-Stroke-Burden)).

### Competition

Boston Scientific (Farapulse, the global PFA leader), J&J (Varipulse, which reached India first ([Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/medical-device/johnson-johnson-brings-indias-first-pfa-technology-for-atrial-fibrillation-treatment-20449))) and Abbott. This is Medtronic's fastest-growing category and the hottest fight in medtech.

**Key terms:** *Atrial fibrillation (AFib)*: a quivering, irregular rhythm of the upper chambers. *Ablation*: deliberately destroying a small area of tissue so it stops carrying signals. *Electroporation*: opening holes in cell walls with short electric pulses, the physics behind PFA.

## 6. Spine implants and the Mazor robot

Spine fusion bolts two or more vertebrae together with screws and rods so a painful or unstable segment stops moving; the Mazor robot guides where each screw goes.

{{< chart src="f06-spine.svg" label="Figure 6" source="Spinal fusion and robot guidance, schematic, not to scale" >}}

### How it works

The spine is a stack of bones (vertebrae) with soft discs between them and the spinal cord running through a canal behind. When a disc wears out or slips, or the spine becomes unstable or deformed, it can pinch nerves and cause back and leg pain. Fusion stops the painful segment from moving.

Surgeons put a screw into each vertebra through the bony 'pedicle', join the screws with a rod, and often place a cage where the disc was so bone grows across and the two vertebrae become one (Figure 6). A screw a few millimetres off can hit a nerve. Mazor plans every screw on the patient's CT scan and holds a guide arm on that exact path. The O-arm (a mobile 3D X-ray) and StealthStation navigation confirm the result during surgery.

### How it's made

Screws and rods are machined from titanium alloy or cobalt-chrome on precision CNC lathes. Cages are PEEK plastic or 3D-printed porous titanium, which bone can grow into. Mazor is a robotic arm plus planning software and a camera system. It is sold as capital equipment, but the money is in the implants each surgery uses.

### Price in India

No reliable public figure for robotic spine fusion was found; hospitals quote after evaluation ([Manipal](https://www.manipalhospitalsglobal.com/treatments/robotic-assisted-lumbar-fusion/)). This is an open question. Mazor X launched in India in 2019 at the Indian Spinal Injuries Centre, Delhi ([Becker's Spine](https://www.beckersspine.com/spinal-tech/medtronic-s-mazor-x-robot-for-spine-surgery-launches-in-india/)).

### Competition

Globus Medical (merged with NuVasive; ExcelsiusGPS robot), Stryker, J&J DePuy Synthes and Zimmer Biomet. In India, local implant makers sell cheaper screw systems. Medtronic is the global spine leader, and the robot helps keep surgeons using its implants: robots' implants are not interchangeable with other brands' ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1092518/indian-hospitals-are-pushing-robotic-surgery-but-do-you-really-need-it)).

**Key terms:** *Vertebra*: one bone of the spine. *Pedicle*: the strong bony bridge at the back of a vertebra that a screw passes through. *Fusion*: joining two vertebrae so they heal as one bone. *PEEK*: a tough medical plastic that X-rays pass through, so doctors can watch the bone heal.

## 7. Neurostimulators (DBS, spinal cord stimulation, InterStim)

These are pacemakers for the nervous system: implanted electrodes send small, steady pulses that calm faulty nerve signals.

{{< chart src="f07-neuro.svg" label="Figure 7" source="DBS and spinal cord stimulation, schematic, not to scale" >}}

### How it works

Nerves carry messages as tiny electrical signals. In some diseases a circuit misfires: in Parkinson's disease, a deep brain circuit produces tremor and stiffness; in chronic pain, the spinal cord keeps sending pain messages long after an injury. A neurostimulator sends small, steady pulses into that circuit to calm it (Figure 7).

Deep brain stimulation (DBS) puts thin electrodes deep in the brain, connected by a wire under the skin to a battery under the collarbone. Medtronic's Percept can also read brain signals and adjust itself. Spinal cord stimulation lays a lead on the spinal cord to block chronic back and leg pain signals before they reach the brain. InterStim stimulates the sacral nerve, low in the back, to control an overactive bladder or bowel.

### How it's made

Electrodes are platinum-iridium rings on thin polyurethane leads. The pulse generator follows the same recipe as a pacemaker: a sealed titanium can with a chip and a battery, rechargeable through the skin in premium models. Doctors program it wirelessly with a tablet.

### Price in India

A DBS procedure costs ₹5 to 16 lakh, of which the device is ₹7 lakh (non-rechargeable) to ₹9 lakh (rechargeable) ([Impact Guru](https://www.impactguru.com/info/deep-brain-stimulation-cost-in-india); [CARE](https://www.carehospitals.com/surgery-cost/deep-brain-stimulation-cost)). The two figures don't fully agree: a ₹5 lakh package cannot include a ₹7 lakh device. The spinal cord stimulator price is an open question.

### Competition

Abbott and Boston Scientific compete in both DBS and spinal cord stimulation. Nevro (now part of Globus) competes in spinal cord stimulation, and Axonics (now part of Boston Scientific) against InterStim. There is no Indian maker.

**Key terms:** *Neuromodulation*: changing nerve activity with electrical pulses. *DBS*: deep brain stimulation. *Rechargeable generator*: a battery topped up wirelessly through the skin, so it lasts many more years before replacement.

## 8. Surgical staplers and energy devices (Endo GIA, Signia, LigaSure)

These replace needle and thread: a stapler cuts and seals tissue in one squeeze, and LigaSure seals blood vessels with heat and pressure.

{{< chart src="f08-staplers.svg" label="Figure 8" source="Surgical stapling and vessel sealing, schematic, not to scale" >}}

### How it works

When a surgeon removes part of the bowel, stomach or lung, or re-routes the stomach in obesity surgery, the cut ends must be closed so nothing leaks, and blood vessels must be shut so they don't bleed. Sewing by hand is slow, especially through keyhole cuts. A stapler lays several rows of tiny staples on each side and a blade cuts between them, so both ends stay closed (Figure 8). Each staple is pressed into a 'B' shape that holds tissue tight without crushing its blood supply. Signia is a powered version that senses tissue thickness and adjusts its speed.

LigaSure grips a vessel, passes radio-frequency current through it and fuses the vessel wall shut, so the surgeon can cut without bleeding.

### How it's made

Staples are formed from titanium wire and loaded into moulded plastic cartridges, each with a knife. The cartridge (reload) is single-use, and one surgery can use several. The handle is precision-moulded plastic and metal; Signia adds a motor and electronics. LigaSure is a generator plus single-use jaws. The model is razor-and-blade: the reloads are the profit.

### Price in India

Indian and Chinese staplers sell for about ₹5,000 to 20,000 each, for example a linear cutter reload at ₹5,160 and a circular stapler at ₹9,435 ([Medikabazaar](https://www.medikabazaar.com/consumables/stapler)). Medtronic's own prices are not public: open question.

### Competition

J&J Ethicon (Echelon staplers, Harmonic scalpel) is the main global rival. In India: Meril, Healthium, Lotus and Axon, plus cheap Chinese imports that undercut on price.

**Key terms:** *Reload*: the single-use cartridge of staples and knife. *Anastomosis*: joining two cut ends of bowel or vessel. *Vessel sealing*: closing a blood vessel with heat and pressure instead of stitches or clips.

## 9. Hugo surgical robot

Hugo lets a surgeon sitting at a console control robotic arms that hold a camera and wristed instruments inside the patient through small cuts.

{{< chart src="f09-hugo.svg" label="Figure 9" source="Hugo operating-room layout, schematic, not to scale" >}}

### How it works

In keyhole (laparoscopic) surgery, the surgeon works through small cuts with long, straight instruments while watching a screen. It is hard on the surgeon, and the instruments can't bend like a wrist. A surgical robot fixes both. The surgeon sits at a console a few metres away, and robotic arms hold the camera and wristed instruments inside the patient (Figure 9). The robot filters out hand tremor and gives a magnified 3D view, so there is less blood loss and recovery is faster than in open surgery. It does nothing on its own; every move comes from the surgeon.

Hugo is used for prostate, kidney, gynaecology, hernia and similar operations.

### How it's made

Hugo is modular. Each arm sits on its own wheeled cart, so a hospital can use 2 to 4 and move them between rooms. There is an open console with 3D glasses, and a tower for vision and power. It has precision motors, cable-driven wristed instruments (reusable a set number of times), a high-definition camera and a lot of software. The money comes from the system, the yearly service and the per-case instruments.

### Price in India

Imported robots cost ₹20 crore and up, plus about ₹1 crore a year in maintenance. A robotic operation costs about ₹1 lakh more than keyhole surgery ([Scroll, May 2026](https://scroll.in/article/1092518/indian-hospitals-are-pushing-robotic-surgery-but-do-you-really-need-it)). Hugo-specific prices are not public. Medtronic launched Hugo in India in September 2021 at its Gurugram training centre ([BioSpectrum](https://biospectrumindia.com/news/97/19572/medtronic-launches-robotic-assisted-surgery-platform-hugo-ras-system-in-india.html)).

### Competition

Intuitive Surgical's da Vinci dominates: about 250 of India's 500+ robots, and about 45,000 of nearly 50,000 robotic cases in 2025 ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1092518/indian-hospitals-are-pushing-robotic-surgery-but-do-you-really-need-it)). Indian challenger **SS Innovations (Mantra)** has sold 160 robots, priced at about a third of da Vinci ([Business Standard, 2022](https://business-standard.com/article/technology/ssi-mantra-india-s-robotic-surgery-system-costs-one-third-of-global-price-122091500958_1.html)). Other makers in India include Meril, Medbot (China), Stryker and CMR (Versius). Hugo is a distant challenger here.

**Key terms:** *Laparoscopy*: keyhole surgery through small cuts. *Console*: the station where the surgeon sits and controls the arms. *Install base*: how many robots are already in hospitals, which drives instrument sales.

## 10. Insulin pumps and glucose sensors (MiniMed 780G)

The 780G is an artificial pancreas, partly automated: a sensor reads sugar every few minutes and a pump adjusts the insulin dose by itself.

{{< chart src="f10-insulin-loop.svg" label="Figure 10" source="Hybrid closed-loop insulin delivery, schematic" >}}

### How it works

In type 1 diabetes the pancreas makes no insulin, the hormone that moves sugar from the blood into cells. Patients must inject insulin several times a day and prick their fingers to check their sugar. Too little insulin and sugar runs high; too much and it crashes dangerously low.

The 780G automates much of this as a loop (Figure 10). A sensor (Guardian 4 or Simplera) sits in the skin and reads glucose. The pump's algorithm raises or cuts insulin every 5 minutes, and a pocket-sized pump pushes it through a thin tube into a tiny cannula under the skin. The user still tells it about meals, which is why it is called a 'hybrid' closed loop.

### How it's made

The pump is a small motor turning a screw that pushes a plunger in an insulin reservoir, with a controller, battery and safety alarms in a sealed case. The sensor is a hair-thin filament coated with an enzyme that reacts with glucose and produces a tiny current, paired with a radio transmitter. The infusion sets, reservoirs and sensors are disposable and changed every few days, which is the recurring revenue.

### Price in India

Tubed pumps cost ₹1.5 to 3 lakh, and hybrid closed-loop systems like the 780G ₹3 to 6 lakh. Sensors and infusion sets are extra and ongoing ([Yashoda](https://www.yashodahospitals.com/procedure-cost/insulin-pump-cost-in-india/)).

### Competition

Insulet (Omnipod, tubeless), Tandem, Roche (Accu-Chek) and Ypsomed make pumps. In sensors: Abbott FreeStyle Libre and Dexcom. Note: this business has been spun off as **MiniMed**, a separate company, and Medtronic is selling its remaining stake.

**Key terms:** *CGM*: continuous glucose monitor, the skin sensor. *Cannula*: the tiny soft tube under the skin that insulin flows through. *Hybrid closed loop*: the pump doses automatically, but the user still enters meals.

## Investor view: where the moats are

The pattern is simple. The harder a device is to make safely inside the body, the less India competes. The more mechanical it is, the faster Indian makers catch up on price.

| Moat type | Devices | Why it holds (or doesn't) | Indian challenger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sealed electronics implanted for years | Pacemakers, ICDs, neurostimulators | Failure can kill, so doctors trust 20+ years of data; electronics are hard to copy | Almost none (Shree Pacetronix at the low end) |
| Razor and blade | Staplers, LigaSure, PFA catheters, robot instruments, glucose sensors | Every case buys new disposables; the console or handle locks the hospital in | Strong in staplers (Meril, Healthium); none yet in PFA |
| Robot lock-in | Mazor, Hugo | The robot pulls through implants and instruments of the same brand | SS Innovations in soft-tissue robots |
| Price-capped | Coronary stents | The government sets one price for all brands, so the brand premium is gone | Meril, SMT, Translumina |
| Tissue plus craft | TAVR | Hand-sewn tissue and clinical trials are slow to copy | Meril Myval already competes; S3V coming |

**Second-order view.** The price cap on stents is a warning for every device that becomes common. The government's trade-margin review of devices is still open, so Medtronic's India profits rest on the devices that are hard to copy (implanted electronics, PFA), not on volume. For Indian stocks, the mechanical categories are where local makers win on price; the companies there are SMT (stents, listed), SS Innovations (robots, US-listed), and the unlisted Meril and Healthium. Watch whether pacemaker or PFA prices get capped next.

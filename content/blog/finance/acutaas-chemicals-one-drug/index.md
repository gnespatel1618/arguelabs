## The story in one minute

Acutaas Chemicals, formerly Ami Organics, is a Surat company that makes the chemical building blocks of medicines. In FY21 it sold ₹341 Cr of mostly generic drug intermediates. In FY26 it sold ₹1,339 Cr at a 36% EBITDA margin, and the stock is up about five times in three years.

Most of that jump came from **one customer and one drug**. Acutaas makes five intermediates for Fermion, the drug-ingredient arm of Finland's Orion. Fermion turns them into darolutamide, which Bayer sells as **Nubeqa** for prostate cancer. Nubeqa's sales went from €869m in 2023 to about €2.4bn in 2025 and are now running at about €3.5bn a year. Acutaas's contract-manufacturing (CDMO) revenue went from about ₹57 Cr to ₹635 Cr in two years.

{{< chart src="a01-at-a-glance.svg" label="At a glance" source="Screener.in, company results and calls · as of 21 Oct 2026" >}}

So the investor's questions are simple:

1. How long can the Nubeqa engine keep running?
2. What else is coming: four new CDMO drugs, battery chemicals and chip chemicals?
3. How much of all that is already in a price of ₹3,152?

**Where we land:** a very good business priced for a near-perfect decade. The core pharma business keeps beating its own guidance. Every new venture so far has arrived late or smaller than promised. Our blended fair value is **₹2,050 to ₹2,450**, about 28% below today's price at the midpoint. The details and the maths are below; the conclusion is at the end.

> **Disclosure:** shares of Acutaas Chemicals are held in my family portfolio, so read this with that bias in mind. This is research, not a recommendation to buy or sell.

## What Acutaas actually makes

A medicine's active ingredient (the API) is built in many chemical steps. Each step's output is an "intermediate". Acutaas makes the advanced intermediates, usually the last few steps before the API, and sells them to the companies that finish the API. It sells about 610 products to about 600 customers in 55 countries. About 76% of revenue is exports.

There are three ways it earns money from this:

- **Generic intermediates.** Acutaas owns the process and sells to many generic drug makers. It claims 50–90% global share in some molecules: dolutegravir (HIV), trazodone (depression), entacapone (Parkinson's), apixaban and rivaroxaban (blood thinners). It is good business, but Chinese suppliers can and do cut prices.
- **Innovator supply and CDMO.** Acutaas makes intermediates for a patented drug under a long contract. The customer owns the drug. This is where the money is now.
- **Specialty chemicals.** Parabens and methyl salicylate (an old, low-margin business being wound down), photoresist chemicals for chips, and from FY27, battery electrolyte additives.

## The industry: a good place to be, at a full price

**Pharma outsourcing is growing faster in India than anywhere else.** Frost & Sullivan (cited in Anthem's IPO papers) sizes India's contract research and manufacturing market at about US$8.2bn in 2024, growing about 13% a year to US$15.4bn by 2029. The global market grows 8–9%. ICRA uses a narrower definition (US$3.4–4.0bn) but sees similar growth of 15–16% a year.

Three things are pushing work to India:

- **China+1.** Big drug companies are moving development work out of China, slowly. The US BIOSECURE Act became law in December 2025, but its bans on Chinese suppliers likely start only around 2028, and existing contracts are protected for years after that. It helps sentiment now, revenue later.
- **Biotech money is back.** Biotech funding rose for four straight quarters to mid-2026. CDMO orders usually follow with a 6–9 month lag.
- **US tariffs spare generics so far.** The April 2026 Section 232 tariffs hit patented drugs, not generics, though the generic exemption comes up for review around April 2027.

Two things push the other way:

- **Orders are lumpy.** In Q1 FY27, Anthem's revenue fell 29%, Cohance's 23% and Blue Jet's 17%, all from shipment timing and customer destocking. Acutaas's own revenue fell 24% from the previous quarter.
- **China still sets generic prices.** In September 2025 Chinese makers cut prices on 41 drug ingredients by 40–50%. Acutaas's generic book has no long-term contracts to protect it.

**The other two businesses sit in harder markets:**

- **Specialty chemicals are in a slump.** CRISIL expects sector revenue to grow only about 6% this year, with margins down 1.5–2 points, because Chinese dumping caps prices. Acutaas's parabens business is exactly this kind of product.
- **Battery additives are a cyclical commodity with a short-term tailwind.** China makes most of the world's vinylene carbonate (VC). Its price collapsed from about 475,000 yuan per tonne in 2021 to 46,000 in 2025, then jumped back above 200,000 in July 2026 after a new Chinese battery-safety rule. Acutaas is India's only listed maker of these additives. Neogen, GFL and Tatva Chintan make lithium salts and electrolyte instead, so they could be customers rather than rivals.

## The customers, and what they are doing

Acutaas rarely names its customers. Brokers, deal announcements and the customers' own reports fill most of the gaps. The picture: one customer drives the growth today, a group of generic drug makers gives a steady base, and the next wave of customers (four drug originators and some battery makers) has signed up but not yet started paying much.

### 1. Fermion, Orion and Bayer: the Nubeqa engine

{{< chart src="a04-nubeqa-chain.svg" label="Figure 6" source="Company announcements, Orion and Bayer reports, patent filings" >}}

Acutaas has supplied Fermion for over a decade. The big change came in November 2022: a 10-year, "multi-tonne" contract for an intermediate of a patented drug, widened in September and December 2023. The company never names the drug. Two brokers (Prabhudas Lilladher and Value Research) say it is darolutamide, and management did not deny it when asked on the July 2026 call. About a third of the Ankleshwar plant is dedicated to it.

The drug is doing very well:

{{< chart src="a05-nubeqa-quarterly.svg" label="Figure 7" source="Bayer quarterly reports · Q1 2022–Q2 2026" >}}

- **Sales:** €869m (2023) to €1,523m (2024) to about €2.4bn (2025). In the first half of 2026 sales grew 61%.
- **New uses:** the US FDA approved a third use, for advanced prostate cancer without chemotherapy (ARANOTE), in June 2025. Two more large trials are running, with new approvals expected around 2028.
- **Orion's outlook:** Orion raised its 2026 guidance in July on higher Nubeqa royalties and says its own Nubeqa income could pass €1bn a year by about 2030.

What this did to Acutaas (broker estimates, because the company stopped disclosing the split):

{{< chart src="n01-cdmo-ramp.svg" label="Figure 8" source="Broker estimates (Prabhudas Lilladher, JM Financial); management target from the Jan 2025 call" >}}

If "the majority" of CDMO is Fermion, the Nubeqa intermediates were roughly **₹380–540 Cr, or 28–40% of FY26 revenue**. At the IPO in 2021, the top customer was 16%. Kotak downgraded the stock to REDUCE in July 2026 for exactly this reason.

**Three things to know about this engine:**

1. **It will slow.** Acutaas's CDMO revenue grew 125% in FY26 while Nubeqa grew 57%, because Acutaas added more intermediates and customers built stock. From here, Acutaas's growth should move toward Nubeqa's volume growth, roughly 15–30% a year and falling.
2. **It is lumpy.** Orion says Fermion's 2026 deliveries are driven by "timing" and should "level out during H2/2026". Acutaas management plans on purchase orders, not on Bayer's sales forecasts. A 10% swing in Fermion orders moves Acutaas revenue by about ₹40–55 Cr.
3. **It has an end date.** Patents expire between 2029 (UK) and 2035 (big EU markets); the US is about 2033. Alembic is already in a US patent fight to launch a generic. After expiry, generic darolutamide makers will still need intermediates, so volume may survive, but the exclusive position and its pricing will not.

The next data point is **Orion's Q3 report on 28 October 2026**.

### 2. Generic drug makers: the steady base

About half of pharma revenue comes from generic intermediates sold to many API makers. Named customers over the years include Laurus, Zydus and Cipla (IPO documents) and GSK, Sun Pharma and Dr Reddy's (Value Research). The molecules matter more than the names:

- **Apixaban (Eliquis):** generics launched in Europe in May 2026; US generics are blocked until April 2028. Management says apixaban "has already started picking up". JM Financial puts the opportunity at about ₹440 Cr.
- **Rivaroxaban (Xarelto):** US generics launched in 2025 with prices down about 90%. That means more volume for intermediate makers, at lower prices.
- **Lumateperone (Caplyta):** Acutaas has a process patent on one intermediate and is qualified with three generic makers, waiting for launch.
- **Older molecules (dolutegravir, trazodone, entacapone):** high market shares, mature demand, and exposed to Chinese price cuts.

### 3. Four new drug originators: the next CDMO wave

Four more CDMO products have been validated with "originators". JM Financial says they are in heart disease and cancer. They are waiting for the customers' drug approvals.

- **Size:** management says each is worth ₹50–100 Cr a year at peak, so ₹200–400 Cr in total. JM models ₹320 Cr by FY30.
- **Timing:** revenue starts in H2 FY27, with the main ramp in FY28.
- **Why it matters:** this is the main way concentration on Nubeqa falls. Management targets ₹1,000 Cr of CDMO revenue by FY28 and says it will "meet or beat" it.
- **Track record:** a second CDMO project was first promised for Q4 FY25 and slipped four times.

### 4. Battery makers in North America and Korea

- **Customers:** none named. Management says they are several cell makers in North America, Korea and other regions, and that no single customer dominates.
- **Contracts:** the whole 4,000-tonne capacity is sold for three years at fixed prices, with raw-material and currency costs passed through.
- **Demand:** management calls it "unprecedented", and sales are limited by plant capacity. Supply started in Q1 FY27, but revenue has not been disclosed.
- **The partner that never came:** in 2023–24 Acutaas planned a joint venture to make electrolyte with "one of the largest electrolyte makers in the world". This was almost certainly Korea's Enchem, but the joint venture never got outside money. The October 2026 "acquisition of Enchem Ami Organics for ₹1 lakh" was only a transfer of an empty subsidiary inside the group. Enchem itself is now in trouble: its auditor has flagged doubt about whether it can keep operating, and it is turning to Chinese energy-storage customers such as CATL.
- **A new partner:** in May 2026, Acutaas gave 10% of its battery subsidiary to A.R.Z Pharma of Israel, its long-time marketing agent for these products.

### 5. Chip makers

- **Baba Fine Chemicals:** once served one main customer, a US partner linked to Heraeus. Revenue was about ₹47 Cr before the 2023 deal and only ₹15.7 Cr in FY26, against a promised ₹200–250 Cr. It recovered from Q4 FY26.
- **Indichem (Korea):** sending samples to customers in Korea, Japan and Taiwan. No supply contract has been disclosed. Revenue is expected from FY28.

### The customer-led growth story, in one table

| Engine | Customer signal | Revenue potential | When | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nubeqa intermediates | Bayer sales +61%; Orion raising guidance | Grows with Nubeqa volume; about ₹400–700 Cr | Now to about 2033 | High near term, falling after 2030 |

Add it up and a path to about ₹3,000 Cr of revenue by FY30 is believable, roughly double FY26's run-rate. That is the bull case. The risk is that one engine (Nubeqa) is doing most of the work while the others are still unproven.

## Management and capital allocation

**Who runs it.** The company was started in 2004 by Nareshkumar Patel, a chemical engineer and now Chairman and Managing Director, with Chetan Vaghasia, who runs operations. They own 17.7% and 15.0%. Bhavin Shah has been CFO since 2022. Abhishek Patel, VP Strategy, gives most of the guidance on calls. There have been no CEO or CFO changes since 2023. The board has four executive and four independent directors. The auditor is Maheshwari & Co., a mid-sized regional firm, reappointed to FY29. The Chairman was paid ₹6.4 Cr in FY26, about 2% of profit.

**How the stake changed.** The promoters' share fell from 39.9% to 32.7% in two steps. Only the second step was a sale:

{{< chart src="a06-shareholding.svg" label="Figure 10" source="BSE shareholding filings · Dec 2023–Sep 2026" >}}

- **2024, dilution:** a ₹99 Cr preferential issue and a ₹400 Cr QIP, both at ₹1,240 before the share split. The promoters sold nothing. The QIP money was spent as promised: ₹250 Cr repaid debt, ₹50 Cr went to capex and solar, and ₹88 Cr to general purposes. There were no deviations. The company has been debt-free since May 2025.
- **May 2025, a sale:** the two founders sold ₹301 Cr of shares at ₹1,115 to Kotak Mutual Fund, about 3.3% of the company. The stock is now 2.8 times that price. Some press reports said 6.6%; that used the pre-split share count and is wrong.
- **Since then:** the promoters have held steady with no pledges. Foreign funds rose from 17% to 23%.

## What the FY26 annual report says

We read the 270-page FY26 annual report (dated 29 August 2026): the letters, the segment pages, the auditor's report and the notes.

**The one thing that matters:** this was the best year in the company's history, and the numbers behind it are clean. Revenue rose 33%, EBITDA 107% and profit 122%. The auditor's opinion is unmodified, with no qualifications and no "emphasis of matter". The only key audit matter is how inventory is valued. Customers pay on time. The weak spots are not in the accounts; they are in where the money is going next.

**Six things a quick reader would miss:**

1. **Receivables are clean.** Of ₹364 Cr owed by customers, 84% was not yet due at year end, and only ₹4 Cr was more than six months overdue. Bad-debt provisions are just ₹1.6 Cr.
2. **A lot of money is waiting to earn.** Capital work in progress jumped from ₹130 Cr to ₹332 Cr (mostly the battery plant and Korea). Until those plants make money, they drag on return on capital.
3. **The ROCE headline is flattering.** The report says "highest-ever RoCE of 39.3%", but that figure leaves out cash and unfinished plants. On Screener's plain basis, ROCE was 32%. Still excellent.
4. **Goodwill doubled to ₹104 Cr,** mostly from Indichem. About ₹56 Cr of older goodwill relates to Baba Fine Chemicals, whose revenue has fallen by two-thirds since purchase. It has not been written down.
5. **Related-party dealings are tiny.** Directors' pay was ₹17.5 Cr; purchases from relatives' firms were ₹1.6 Cr. Abhishek Patel, who leads the earnings calls, is listed as a permanent employee and not as a relative of the directors.
6. **Disputed tax claims are small.** ₹17.3 Cr, mostly income-tax demands, about 1% of net worth.

**Profit versus cash**

### Latest call: Q1 FY27, 24 July 2026

**Call tone: POSITIVE** (on a five-step scale from STRONGLY POSITIVE to NEGATIVE)

| Signal | Status | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Result quality | Strong | Revenue +59% and EBITDA +122% on last year; both beat broker estimates |
| Management tone | Confident but guarded | Battery demand "unprecedented", but no battery revenue or customer names given |
| Guidance | Maintained | 25% growth at about 36% margin for FY27, not raised |

**Why revenue fell 24% from Q4:** management says Q1 is always the weakest quarter, with about 40% of revenue in the first half and 60% in the second. Last year's Q1 drop was steeper, at 33%. About half of the margin fall (42% to 34%) came from product mix, likely fewer Nubeqa shipments, and half from fixed costs spread over less revenue. We found no sign of a lost customer.

**Other things from the call:**

- Net cash rose to ₹314 Cr.
- Working capital rose from 91 to 99 days.
- Sachin is at 83% use and land is being bought for expansion.
- Battery product 3 is due by end-Q2 FY27, and product 4's contract is nearly signed.
- Management will not make electrolyte or lithium salts, only additives.

**Call summary:** Q1 looked worse than it was. Revenue fell 24% from a record Q4 but grew 59% on last year, in line with a seasonal pattern management had flagged. Margin fell to 34% on mix and fixed costs, not on any visible problem. Guidance of 25% growth at about 36% margin was kept, not raised. To hit it, the rest of the year needs only about 19% growth, so it looks conservative. The stock still fell 6% that day because investors wanted a raise. Two caveats. Management admitted revenue concentration in one product rose, and it will not give battery revenue. The next checks are the **Q2 results (due by 14 Nov 2026)**, the first quarter with real battery sales, and Orion's Q3 report on 28 October for Nubeqa deliveries.

## Financials and earnings quality

{{< chart src="a09-quarterly.svg" label="Figure 15" source="Screener.in consolidated quarterly results · Jun 2023–Jun 2026" >}}

**Growth.** Revenue went from ₹341 Cr (FY21) to ₹1,339 Cr (FY26), 32% a year compounded. Profit grew 46% a year over the same five years. FY24 was the bad year: Chinese price cuts hit the generic book, margins fell to 18%, and a ₹32 Cr write-off of a US drug-discovery venture cut reported profit to ₹49 Cr.

**Margins.** EBITDA margin rose from 18% (FY24) to 36% (FY26), with a peak of 42% in Q4 FY26. Most of the rise is mix: more high-value CDMO and less commodity chemistry. Solar power and dropping low-margin products also helped. Management guides FY27 margins "similar to FY26", not the 42% peak. That is sensible: battery chemicals will dilute margins as they grow.

## Valuation

**Verdict: OVERVALUED.** Our fair value is **₹2,050–2,450 (midpoint ₹2,260)**, against a price of ₹3,152. The stock is about 40% above our midpoint.

{{< chart src="a10-football-field.svg" label="Figure 18" source="Our P/E and DCF models; broker reports; Screener.in · as of 21 Oct 2026" >}}

### How we valued it

This is a pharma intermediates and CDMO business. Profits are real and growing fast, but they lean on one contract that has an end date.

- **Primary method: P/E on FY28 earnings.** Earnings are clean and growing about 20% a year, and this is how the market and peers price CDMO companies.
- **Cross-check: discounted cash flow (DCF).** A DCF forces the question of how much cash the business will actually throw off after paying for its plants.
- **Weighting:** 70% P/E, 30% DCF.
- **Not used:** price to book (assets are not the point when ROCE is 32%), price to sales (it ignores margins), and a sum-of-the-parts. Kotak uses a sum-of-the-parts and values the non-Nubeqa business at 35x.

## Risks

{{< chart src="a11-risk-map.svg" label="Figure 23" source="Risk map · placed by judgement" >}}

**Downside risks** (impact on our ₹2,260 fair value; probability over two years)

- **Nubeqa orders flatten.** Orion says Fermion's 2026 deliveries were "timing" and should level out in H2 2026. If Acutaas's Nubeqa revenue grows 10% instead of 25% a year, FY28 EPS drops by about ₹6–7. Impact: about −₹200 (−9%). Probability: medium. Watch: Orion's quarterly Fermion and Nubeqa figures, and the June-quarter dip.
- **The multiple falls.** At 67x trailing earnings, any miss can take the P/E toward the low 40s, as happened to Anthem and Cohance after weak quarters. Impact: on the share price, not on fair value; a 45x P/E on FY27 earnings is about ₹2,340. Probability: medium to high. Watch: Q2 results.
- **New CDMO drugs slip.** All four depend on customers' approvals, and the last new CDMO launch slipped four times. Impact: about −₹150 (−7%). Probability: medium. Watch: first revenue in H2 FY27.
- **Early generic darolutamide.** Alembic is in a US patent fight. An early win would cut the Nubeqa stream's life. Impact: about −₹400 (−18%). Probability: low. Watch: US court rulings.
- **Fermion dual-sources.** A big customer with one supplier for five intermediates may add a second. Impact: about −₹300 (−13%). Probability: low to medium. Watch: Ankleshwar use and gross margin.
- **Battery prices reset.** VC prices fell about 90% from 2021 to 2025 before recovering. The three-year contracts roll over around FY29. Impact: about −₹60 (−3%). Probability: medium.
- **Tax search.** No demand has been disclosed yet. Impact: small unless it uncovers a pattern. Probability: low.

**Upside risks**

- **Nubeqa beats again.** Q2 2026 already runs above Bayer's €3bn peak target, and two more uses could be approved around 2028. Impact: +₹300–500.
- **A second big CDMO contract.** Value Research reported a deal with "another large European originator" in final negotiation in April 2026. Impact: +₹200–400 and lower concentration.
- **Battery prices stay high.** China's VC price is up about fourfold from its 2025 low. New contracts at these prices would make the battery plant far more profitable than we assume. Impact: +₹100–200.

## Conclusion

Acutaas is one of the best-run chemistry businesses in its size class.

- **It wins in its core.** It took a niche intermediates business, won a 10-year contract on one of the fastest-growing cancer drugs in the world, and doubled its margins. It is debt-free. Its accounts are clean. On the core business it beats its own guidance year after year.
- **It struggles outside the core.** A written-off drug-discovery venture, a chip-chemicals buy that shrank, an electrolyte joint venture that never happened, and a battery plant two years late.

Today's price assumes the core keeps compounding for a decade and the new ventures finally work.

> **In one line:** a great business that depends on one drug, priced as if it were already a broad-based CDMO.
